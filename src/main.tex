\begin{abstract}
    Coordinating traffic-signal agents in multi-agent reinforcement learning
    requires partitioning intersections into coordination modules before
    training, yet existing practice either inherits partitions from
    road-topology tools or learns them end-to-end for control return,
    without a principled account of what a partition should preserve and
    why.
    This paper develops a training-free, retention-based theory connecting
    stationary-walk escape and empirical flow retention to the flow-mediated
    component of regional multi-agent reinforcement learning value loss, and
    operationalizes it with a dependency retention metric, size- and
    connectivity-matched null models, and a constrained, held-out
    community-detection benchmark.
    Across two simulated road networks and every matched module count,
    flow-based partitions retain 15 to 33 percentage points more flow and 3
    to 34 percentage points more state dependency than the standard
    graph-partitioning baseline METIS, and both structured partitions clear
    a randomly assigned baseline by a wider margin still, matching the
    predicted retention ordering in every comparison.
    A preregistered randomized intervention then finds no resolvable causal
    coupling between regions in the tested traffic regime, so the
    accompanying single-seed training gains are treated as consistent with
    improved optimization conditioning from correlated within-module signals,
    and this evidentiary split is stated explicitly.
    A benchmark spanning several partitioning algorithms further shows how to
    apply the same retention criterion under operational feasibility
    constraints, with InfoMap serving as the natural flow-retaining
    instantiation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Urban traffic signal control (TSC) is a canonical large-scale coordination
problem: a phase decision at one intersection reshapes arrival patterns and
queue spill-back at its neighbors, so signals must be controlled jointly,
yet exact joint control requires evaluating a joint action space that grows
exponentially with the number of intersections, which is intractable beyond
a handful of
agents~\citep{nair2005ndpomdp,kok2006ndpomdp,oliehoek2012ndpomdp}.
This is why regional decomposition, not monolithic joint control, is the
paradigm that makes multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) practical at
city scale~\citep{qu2020average,qu2022scalable}: practical systems partition
the network into coordination modules, whether explicit regions or implicit
neighborhoods, and coordinate \emph{within} them, by shared rewards,
centralized critics, communication, or
hierarchy~\citep{ma2020fma2c,wei2019colight,xu2021hilight,zhu2026halo}.
Every such system thus contains a \emph{coordination graph} fixed before
training: a decision about which agents share coordination machinery.
Unlike domains where physics dictates the interaction structure, in TSC this
graph is a free design variable (Section~\ref{sec:ndpomdp}), and its design
has received far less attention than the learning algorithms that run on
top of it.

When the coordination graph is designed at all, it is typically inherited
from general-purpose partitioners applied to road \emph{topology}, learned
end-to-end for control return without stating what a good partition should
retain, or borrowed from transport-planning zoning that is not connected to
what learning agents require of a
partition~\citep{laplacian2009ieee,communitydetection2021plosone,liu2023gplight,ma2022feudal,odflow2019partition,mfdpartition2020}.
Attention-based MARL~\citep{jiang2018atoc,wei2019colight} learns interaction
structure implicitly instead, but gives no account of which dependencies are
preserved or severed when attention is restricted to modules.
The gap spanning all three lines of work is the rationale itself:
\emph{no prior work connects network structure, information theory, and
    MARL performance into a principled account of which partitioning
    criterion to choose and why}.
Partition choice is consequential, routinely made, and, in current practice,
unjustified.

This paper supplies that account by changing the question from \emph{which
    partitioner} to \emph{what a partition should preserve}.
Coordination pressure in traffic networks is carried by vehicle flow, so an
OD-induced signal-transition flow graph is the natural partitioning
substrate, and the map equation~\citep{rosvall2008maps} gives a useful
information-theoretic candidate objective.
We develop a three-link \emph{retention chain} connecting stationary-walk
escape and empirical flow retention to the flow-mediated component of
regional MARL value loss
(Section~\ref{sec:method}), making flow retention a principled,
\emph{training-free} surrogate for coordination quality, and measure it with
a dependency retention ratio instantiated by three complementary instruments
across a coordination coupling spectrum that holds the partition fixed while
varying the coordination mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:levels}).

Our two most consequential contributions, with evidentiary status stated
explicitly:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A retention-based theory of partition quality}
          (Sections~\ref{sec:method}--\ref{sec:psi}): the first formal chain
          connecting stationary-walk escape and empirical flow retention to
          the flow-mediated component of regional-MARL value loss, with an
          explicit criterion for how much flow and influence a partition
          retains, statable and auditable independently of any learner,
          together with the standardized metrics and null models needed to
          measure it.
    \item \textbf{Confirmatory evidence and an evaluation frame, not an
              algorithm} (Sections~\ref{sec:exp-phi}--\ref{sec:exp-benchmark}):
          flow-based partitions retain more flow and more state dependency
          than METIS across every matched module count, two networks, and
          three seeds; a preregistered randomized intervention finds no
          resolvable causal coupling in the tested regime, so the
          accompanying single-seed training gains are reported as consistent
          with \emph{optimization conditioning} rather than severed coupling;
          and
          a constrained, held-out community-detection benchmark shows how to
          apply the retention criterion under deployment constraints, with
          InfoMap serving as the natural flow-retaining instantiation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Network partitioning for traffic control.}
Partitioning is already standard machinery in large-scale TSC, but the
criterion is almost always structural or geometric: spectral partitioning of
the road graph~\citep{laplacian2009ieee}, a manually assumed region
decomposition in feudal MARL~\citep{ma2020fma2c}, adjacency-constrained
star-topology regions~\citep{regionlight2024}, or congestion-feature and
learned-similarity
clustering~\citep{communitydetection2021plosone,liu2023gplight}.
The closest departure, \citet{ma2022feudal}, learns a flow-based
decomposition end-to-end for a feudal learner, but optimizes directly for
control return, yielding no criterion that can be stated or audited
independently of the learner.
Flow-aware partitioning exists in the transport
literature~\citep{odflow2019partition,mfdpartition2020}, but serves zoning
and macroscopic modeling, not learning agents.
Our gap claim is deliberately narrow: not that flow-based partitioning is
new, nor that it has never been coupled to a learner, but that no prior work
provides an \emph{explicit partition-quality criterion}, statable before
training and independent of any learner, for what a coordination-graph
designer should optimize.

\paragraph{Coordination topology in MARL.}
A complementary literature learns or fixes interaction structure inside the
learner instead of the coordination graph outside it: attentional and
inferred communication~\citep{jiang2018atoc,i2c2021,peng2020structured,aamas2021correlated},
graph attention over road topology~\citep{wei2019colight}, and
information-bottleneck or scalability-oriented
topologies~\citep{hetib2025,sun2026ibvq,wang2020imac,expocomm2025,li2025topology,zhu2026halo},
which discover useful structure implicitly but give no external account of
what is preserved or severed, since it lives inside the model and cannot be
audited before training.
Our approach is complementary rather than competing: we fix the partition
from a training-free criterion and use the trained models' own dependency
structure (Section~\ref{sec:psi}) to evaluate the partition after the fact.

\paragraph{Measuring inter-agent dependency.}
We adapt three existing measurement families to a new question: model-free
mutual information between agent states, whose mechanistic link to
spill-back in TSC has been made explicit~\citep{dependencydyn2025};
Jacobian-based influence profiles from GNN and attention-attribution
diagnostics~\citep{lrd2025,abnar2020rollout,simonyan2013saliency,sundararajan2017axiomatic};
and a retention ratio that aggregates any such matrix into a partition-level
score against size-matched, connectivity-preserving nulls, which we did not
find elsewhere in the MARL literature.

\paragraph{Theory imports.}
The theoretical chain connects three literatures that have not previously
met: the map equation and Markov stability in network
science~\citep{rosvall2008maps,rosvall2009mapequation,delvenne2010stability,lambiotte2014multiscale},
exponential-decay value-locality and influence-based-abstraction bounds in
networked MARL~\citep{qu2020average,qu2022scalable,congeduti2021iba}, and
cell-transmission/link-queue traffic
dynamics~\citep{daganzo1994ctm,jin2012linkqueue}, none of which alone links
stationary-walk escape, flow-mediated influence, and regional-MARL value
loss.

\paragraph{Community-detection baselines.}
Our benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:exp-benchmark}) draws its candidates from the
standard repertoire: normalized cut~\citep{shimalik2000},
Leiden~\citep{traag2019leiden,traag2011cpm}, Markov stability,
METIS~\citep{karypis1998metis}, and Infomap.
We claim no new detector, only the harness under which we find that none
dominates.

\section{Retention Framework}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Coordination Graph as a Design Variable}
\label{sec:ndpomdp}

Each signalized intersection $i \in \mathcal{V}$ observes local queues,
phase, and elapsed phase time $o_i$, and selects a phase action
$a_i \in \mathcal{A}_i$.
We model TSC as a network-distributed POMDP whose coordination graph
$\mathcal{G}_C=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}_C)$ determines which agents share
explicit coordination machinery.
The reward decomposes into local and interaction terms,
\begin{equation}
    R(\mathbf{a})
    =
    \sum_i r_i(o_i,a_i)
    +
    \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_C}
    r_{ij}(o_i,o_j,a_i,a_j).
    \label{eq:ndpomdp}
\end{equation}
Exact coordination over $\omega$ agents scales as $|\mathcal{A}|^\omega$,
so practical MARL systems bound module size and decide which edges to keep
before training.
In traffic, the cost of cutting an edge is governed by vehicle movement:
high-volume corridors create arrival coupling and, near saturation,
spill-back coupling.
The partitioning problem is therefore to keep the coordination graph small
while retaining the vehicle flow that carries those dependencies.

This framing separates three objects that are often blurred.
The \emph{road graph} says which intersections are physically adjacent.
The \emph{OD-induced signal-transition flow graph} says which consecutive
signalized intersections exchange vehicles under a demand regime.
The \emph{coordination graph} says which agents share rewards, critic inputs,
or messages during learning.
Topology is useful, but it is not the same as coordination pressure: two
adjacent intersections with low traffic exchange may need little explicit
coordination, while a high-volume corridor can carry dependency even when
the relevant decision pressure is not captured by an unweighted adjacency
cut.
The role of the partition is to turn this route-flow graph into a
coordination graph whose modules are small enough for MARL and coherent
enough that important dependencies remain inside modules.

The same distinction also explains why a partition can help even when
causal inter-agent action effects are unresolved in a particular test regime.
Regional reward sharing and regional critics do not require every within
module pair to exert a measurable causal effect at the next decision step.
They require signals that are jointly informative for learning.
Correlated queue and delay trajectories can reduce reward noise and critic
approximation burden even when the closed-loop controller damps the direct
action-to-queue channel.
The framework below therefore keeps two terms separate: the coupling term
that the retention theory bounds, and the optimization-conditioning term
that the experiments treat as a leading working interpretation.

\subsection{Flow Retention and InfoMap}
\label{sec:infomap}
\label{sec:theory}

Let $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{flow}}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E},\mathbf{W})$ be
the OD-induced signal-transition flow graph, where $W_{ij}$ is the number of
vehicles traveling from signal $i$ to the next signal $j$ along realized
routes during a representative warm-up window.
We construct $\mathbf{W}$ from simulation trip logs by recording the
sequence of signalized intersections visited by each completed trip and
incrementing the corresponding directed OD edge counts.
The warm-up policy is uniformly random, so the resulting graph reflects
demand and network geometry before a learned controller reshapes the flow.
This is deliberately training-free: the partition can be chosen before any
MARL run, and its quality can be audited without conflating partition choice
with optimization luck.
For a partition $\mathcal{P}=\{P_1,\ldots,P_K\}$, define empirical OD-flow
retention as
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_W(\mathcal{P})
    =
    \frac{\sum_k\sum_{i,j\in P_k} W_{ij}}
    {\sum_{i,j} W_{ij}} .
    \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
We write $\Phi$ for $\Phi_W$ in the empirical tables.
For another non-negative edge-weight matrix $\mathbf{X}$,
$\Phi_W(\mathcal{P};\mathbf{X})$ denotes Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi} with
$\mathbf{W}$ replaced by $\mathbf{X}$.

InfoMap~\citep{rosvall2008maps,rosvall2009mapequation} is a natural fit
because its coding objective favors, but does not uniquely optimize, the
same flow-retentive structure this hypothesis wants to preserve.
For the random walk used by the map equation,
\begin{align}
    L(\mathcal{P})
     & =
    q_{\circlearrowleft}H(\mathcal{Q})
    +\sum_k p_k^{\circlearrowleft}H(\mathcal{P}_k),
    \label{eq:mapeq} \\
    q_{\circlearrowleft}
     & =
    1-\Phi_\pi(\mathcal{P}),
    \label{eq:qphi}
\end{align}
where
$\Phi_\pi(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{c(i)=c(j)}\pi_iP_{ij}$ is within-module
stationary-walk mass.
Thus the escape term minimized by InfoMap is the complement of retained
stationary flow.
The empirical $\Phi_W$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi} equals $\Phi_\pi$ when the OD
edge measure matches the stationary edge measure, for example under flow
balance and unmodified transition rates.
Otherwise the discrepancy is the measurable walk-mismatch term
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon_{\mathrm{walk}}(\mathcal{P})
    =
    |\Phi_\pi(\mathcal{P})-\Phi_W(\mathcal{P})|.
    \label{eq:walkmismatch}
\end{equation}
This is the useful coincidence of the paper: InfoMap's objective and the
retention hypothesis point in the same direction, so InfoMap supplies a
training-free flow-retaining candidate whose retained mass can be checked
directly.
In plain terms, InfoMap gives short descriptions to walks that stay inside
modules and pays an extra codebook cost when they leave.
If vehicles mostly circulate within a proposed region, that region is cheap
to describe; if vehicles frequently cross a proposed boundary, the boundary
is expensive.
This is exactly the partition property the hypothesis asks to test.
The entropy terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mapeq} prevent the trivial solution of one
giant module, while the measured $\Phi_W$ reports how much observed traffic
the final modules actually retain.

\begin{proposition}[Stationary-walk retention identity]
    \label{prop:phi}
    For the stationary edge measure $\pi_iP_{ij}$ of the walk evaluated by
    Eq.~\eqref{eq:mapeq}, the aggregate inter-module transition rate is
    $q_{\circlearrowleft}=1-\Phi_\pi(\mathcal{P})$.
    For the empirical OD edge measure
    $\mu^W_{ij}=W_{ij}/\sum_{a,b}W_{ab}$,
    $\Phi_W(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{c(i)=c(j)}\mu^W_{ij}$.
    Thus $\Phi_W=\Phi_\pi$ only when the empirical OD edge measure equals
    the stationary edge measure; otherwise
    $1-\Phi_W\leq1-\Phi_\pi+\epsilon_{\mathrm{walk}}$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{OD-Demand Flow Partition Construction}
\label{sec:odconstruction}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:od-demand} makes the training-free construction explicit.
The key step is to convert OD demand into a directed signal-to-signal flow
matrix before any learner is trained.
In simulation, the route weights come from completed SUMO trips; in a field
deployment, they can come from historical assignment or a calibrated route
choice model.
The road graph is used to project routes and enforce connected modules, but
the partitioning substrate is the OD-induced signal-transition matrix
$\mathbf{W}$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
    \caption{OD-induced route-flow partition construction}
    \label{alg:od-demand}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require OD demand $\{(o,d,q_{od})\}$, routes, signals $\mathcal{V}$, feasible counts $\mathcal{K}$
        \State Initialize $W_{ij}\gets0$ for all $i,j\in\mathcal{V}$ and candidate set $\mathcal{C}\gets\emptyset$
        \For{each OD pair $(o,d,q_{od})$}
        \For{each assigned route $r$ with route share $\alpha_r$}
        \State Project $r$ to signal sequence $(v_1,\ldots,v_m)$
        \For{each consecutive pair $(v_\ell,v_{\ell+1})$}
        \State $W_{v_\ell v_{\ell+1}}\gets W_{v_\ell v_{\ell+1}}+\alpha_r q_{od}$
        \EndFor
        \EndFor
        \EndFor
        \For{each $k\in\mathcal{K}$}
        \State Run directed InfoMap on $\mathbf{W}$ and collect $k$-module candidates
        \State Keep candidates satisfying size and road-connectivity limits
        \State Add the feasible candidate with largest $\Phi_W(\mathcal{P})$ to $\mathcal{C}$
        \EndFor
        \State \Return flow-based candidate partitions $\mathcal{C}$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

When completed trips are available, each trip simply contributes one unit of
demand to its realized route, so $\alpha_r q_{od}=1$ for that record.
When only an aggregate OD table is available, $q_{od}$ is split over one or
more assigned routes.
In both cases, the output is the same object: a directed route-flow graph
whose within-module mass is scored by Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi}.

\subsection{Dependency Retention}
\label{sec:psi}

A partition that keeps flow should also keep the dependencies a learner can
use.
For any non-negative dependency matrix $\mathbf{D}$, define
\begin{equation}
    \Psi(\mathcal{P},\mathbf{D})
    =
    \frac{\sum_k\sum_{i,j\in P_k} D_{ij}}
    {\sum_{i,j}D_{ij}} ,
    \label{eq:psi}
\end{equation}
the fraction of measured dependency retained within modules.
For the theoretical chain, the dependency object is an influence matrix, not
an empirical MI matrix.
Let $\Gamma_{ij}:=C_{j\leftarrow i}$ be the one-step influence of source
$i$ on target $j$, and define
\begin{equation}
    \Psi_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{P})
    =
    \frac{\sum_k\sum_{i,j\in P_k} \Gamma_{ij}}
    {\sum_{i,j}\Gamma_{ij}} .
    \label{eq:psigamma}
\end{equation}
The empirical scores $\Psi(\mathcal{P},\mathbf{D})$ below audit this
influence-retention story with measurable matrices; they are not the object
that enters the value-loss bound.
We use three instruments.
$D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ is lagged KSG mutual information between per-agent
traffic-state slices, not the full network state vector, over
$\tau\in\{1,5,15,30\}$ and is the model-free instrument for regional
reward sharing.
It is the right L1 instrument because independent actors have no cross-agent
input path: a Jacobian across agents would be structurally zero even if their
traffic states are highly related.
$D^{G}$ is the absolute input-output Jacobian of a global CoLight model and
audits what a trained attention model depends on.
It must be computed on a global, no-partition checkpoint; computing it on a
partitioned model would bake in cross-module zeros and then rediscover the
partition by construction.
$D^{\mathrm{CMI}}$ is conditional mutual information from one agent's action
to another's future queue, conditioning on the target's own queue and action;
it targets causal control coupling but is treated as a diagnostic because the
available data do not resolve significant edges.
Together, the three instruments cover a useful spectrum: state correlation
for reward conditioning, learned model sensitivity for attention, and
action-level causal influence for the strongest mechanistic claim.

Because coarse or uneven partitions retain more mass mechanically, we also
use a chance-adjusted score
\begin{equation}
    \Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}
    =
    \frac{\Psi-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{R}}[\Psi]}
    {1-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{R}}[\Psi]},
    \label{eq:psiadj}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{R}$ is a null family with the same module-size sequence, and
in the benchmark also the same physical-connectivity constraint.
The former controls for size; the latter controls for the fact that any
connected traffic region already retains more dependency than an arbitrary
assignment.
This matters most for small networks, where one large module and several
singletons can score well on raw retention simply because the large module
contains many pairs.
Raw $\Psi$ answers whether a particular deployed partition keeps dependency;
$\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}$ asks whether it keeps more dependency than partitions
with the same size and operability constraints.
The within/cross MI check, spill-back check, and action-perturbation probe
are therefore read as diagnostics.
They ask, respectively, whether within-module MI exceeds cross-module MI,
whether spill-back remains within modules, and whether randomized actions
produce measurable downstream influence.
They clarify mechanism, while $\Phi$, $\Psi$, and $\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}$ carry
the partition-level claim.

\subsection{From Retention to Regional MARL}
\label{sec:transfer}
\label{sec:valueloss}

The retention chain has three links.
Its scope is structural: it concerns the error introduced by severing
inter-agent influence, not a guarantee that flow retention alone improves
learned control.
The theoretical track is flow to $\Gamma$ to regional value-loss bound and
does not require mutual information.
MI and model Jacobians enter as empirical instruments for auditing retained
dependency.
The value-loss result controls the coupling component of regional
decomposition, while partition-dependent optimization conditioning remains a
separate term.
Finally, the transfer model is an undersaturated approximation and does not
by itself prove nonlinear spill-back.

First, Proposition~\ref{prop:phi} links InfoMap's escape term to
stationary-walk retention, with empirical OD retention connected through
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{walk}}$.
Second, in flow-local traffic dynamics, the influence crossing a partition
is bounded by the corridor-flow exposure it cuts:
\begin{proposition}[Corridor exposure bounds stylized cut influence]
    \label{prop:transfer}
    Under stylized flow-local dynamics, one-step coupling satisfying
    $C_{j\leftarrow i}\leq\alpha\widetilde{W}_{ij}+\beta_{ij}$, and a
    stationary demand regime, there exist constants $\kappa>0$ and
    $\epsilon_{\mathrm{dem}}\geq0$ such that
    \begin{equation}
        1-\Psi_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{P})
        \leq
        \kappa\!\left(1-\Phi_W(\mathcal{P};\widetilde{\mathbf{W}})\right)
        +\epsilon_{\mathrm{dem}},
        \label{eq:transfer}
    \end{equation}
    where $\widetilde{W}_{ij}=W_{ij}+W_{ji}$.
\end{proposition}
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} states the proof status of the stylized
linear link-queue argument and the limits of the bound.
The symmetrized quantity is a bidirectional corridor-flow exposure envelope,
not a mechanistic spill-back variable.
Reverse-direction traffic $W_{ji}$ is not interpreted as downstream-induced
blocking; directed spill-back would require a separate blocked-sending or
storage-state term.
Common rush-hour demand can still correlate far-apart queues, which is why
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{dem}}$ remains explicit.
Third, networked-MARL value-locality and influence-based-abstraction
results~\citep{qu2020average,qu2022scalable,congeduti2021iba} imply that a
regional learner loses value in proportion to the influence it cuts:
\begin{equation}
    J^\star-J_{\mathcal{P}}
    \leq
    C(1-\Psi_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{P}))
    +\epsilon_{\mathrm{approx}}
    +\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}^0
    +\epsilon_{\mathrm{cond}}(\mathcal{P}).
    \label{eq:valueloss}
\end{equation}
Here $C$ is fixed for a network and demand regime,
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{approx}}$ is function-approximation error,
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}^0$ is the partition-independent training floor, and
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{cond}}(\mathcal{P})$ is the partition-dependent
conditioning term.
The bound is one-directional: high influence retention is a sufficient
structural certificate for tightening the coupling-loss term, while low
retention only weakens that guarantee and does not prove poor performance.
Under stationary-walk alignment, flow-local coupling, and networked-MARL
locality, reducing the map-equation escape term tightens the flow-mediated
component of Eq.~\eqref{eq:valueloss}; it does not bound the optimization
term.

\noindent\textbf{Main hypothesis.}
A partition that retains more OD flow should retain more
coordination-relevant dependency, and this retained dependency should support
better regional MARL behavior when the learner can use it.
Equations~\eqref{eq:qphi}--\eqref{eq:valueloss} give the chain:
OD flow defines the coordination pressure; InfoMap reduces stationary-walk
escape and is audited by empirical OD retention; retained corridor-flow
exposure bounds retained influence in the stylized model; retained influence
reduces the coupling term regional MARL cuts away.
When the causal coupling term is not resolved in the tested demand regime,
the same retained state dependency is consistent with an
optimization-conditioning mechanism through correlated rewards and smaller
critic inputs.

\subsection{Coordination Coupling Spectrum}
\label{sec:levels}

We hold the partition fixed while varying the coordination mechanism.
This separates the partition criterion from the learner that uses it.

\begin{table}[t]
    \centering
    \caption{Coordination levels and dependency instruments.}
    \label{tab:levels}
    \footnotesize
    \begin{tabular}{llll}
        \toprule
        Level & Mechanism          & Learner & Instrument                         \\
        \midrule
        L1    & Regional reward    & IPPO    & $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$                  \\
        L2    & Regional critic    & MAPPO   & $\Psi(D^{\mathrm{MI}})$ case study \\
        L3    & Regional attention & CoLight & $D^{G}$                            \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

L1 uses regional reward averaging; it benefits when agents in a module have
correlated outcomes.
L2 uses a regional critic; it benefits when the critic keeps the dominant
state dependencies while reducing input dimension.
The existing MAPPO checkpoints do not expose a target-indexed pairwise
$D^Q$, so L2 performance is interpreted against the model-free dependency
retention and reported as a case study.
L3 uses graph attention; it is sensitive when modules become smaller than the
one-hop influence range, so the $D^G$ result is expected to expose a
module-size boundary rather than always favor flow.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
    \caption{Retention-guided coordination-graph construction}
    \label{alg:workflow}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require Trip logs, road graph, feasible module counts
        \State Build Flow candidates using Algorithm~\ref{alg:od-demand}
        \State Generate additional topology and community-detection candidates
        \State Enforce module-count, size, and connectivity constraints
        \State Estimate dependency matrices on validation traces
        \State Score candidates by $\Phi$, $\Psi$, and $\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}$
        \State Select a small validation-front subset for policy training
        \State Report held-out retention and seed-explicit training results
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:workflow} is the intended use of the framework.
InfoMap is the first candidate because its objective includes the
stationary-walk escape term aligned with retained flow, but the final choice
is made by the same retention score under the same operational constraints
that the controller must satisfy.
This keeps the scientific claim and the engineering procedure aligned:
the paper proves and measures a criterion, then uses candidate partitioners
as ways to instantiate that criterion.

\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate on two SUMO networks: Cologne8, with 8 signalized intersections
and a 30-dimensional total network state, and Ingolstadt21, with 21
signalized intersections and an 85-dimensional total network state.
For each network we compare InfoMap flow partitions, METIS topology
partitions, and a reproducible size-balanced random partition at matched
module count: $k\in\{3,5\}$ on Cologne8 and $k\in\{4,5\}$ on Ingolstadt21.
$D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ uses three stochastic warm-up seeds per network; $D^G$
uses one seed-7 global CoLight checkpoint per network and therefore has no
checkpoint uncertainty estimate.
Downstream IPPO and MAPPO training uses seed~0 for 1500 episodes and is
reported as a descriptive case study, consistent with the evidence standards
for RL evaluation~\citep{henderson2018matters,agarwal2021rliable}.
The benchmark uses 10 stochastic-policy episodes at each of 5 traffic seeds
per network, split chronologically into construction, validation, and
held-out test traces.
The state-MI matrices are estimated from per-agent state slices of 361-step
warm-up trajectories with KSG $k=3$ and lags 1, 5, 15, and 30.
Because KSG on autocorrelated traffic traces is noisy, the evidence uses
partition-level retained mass and seed-wise ordering rather than isolated
pairwise MI magnitudes.
The learned-dependency matrices use the same partition-independent rule on
both networks: take a global CoLight checkpoint, differentiate valid target
outputs with respect to every source agent's input features, and aggregate
absolute sensitivities into a directed non-negative matrix.
The action-level perturbation probe is separate from these retention
diagnostics.
It freezes the global CoLight policy and randomizes actions at execution
time, so it tests closed-loop causal influence without retraining.
All main retention comparisons are matched by map and module count.
The random baseline is a single reproducible round-robin assignment after a
seeded shuffle, so it gives a fixed chance floor rather than a distribution
over random partitions.
The benchmark later replaces this simple control with exact-size and
connectivity-preserving null families.
For readability, the main tables report gains over baselines; raw values are
omitted unless they are needed to interpret a diagnostic.
Retention scores are bounded fractions, so we report their differences in
percentage points rather than ratios:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{pp}}(S)
    =
    100\,[S(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{Flow}})
        -S(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{base}})] .
    \label{eq:gainpp}
\end{equation}
Waiting time is an unbounded lower-is-better cost, so we report percent
reduction:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{wait}}
    =
    100\,\frac{\mathrm{Wait}_{\mathrm{base}}
        -\mathrm{Wait}_{\mathrm{Flow}}}
    {\mathrm{Wait}_{\mathrm{base}}}.
    \label{eq:waitgain}
\end{equation}
This keeps the tables visually consistent: positive values mean Flow retains
more mass or achieves lower waiting time than the named baseline.

\subsection{Result 4: Feasible Selection and Causal Diagnostics}
\label{sec:exp-benchmark}
\label{sec:exp-perturb}

The constrained benchmark asks how the retention criterion behaves once
deployment constraints are enforced.
Across 337,575 candidate records from InfoMap, agent-level InfoMap,
normalized cut, Leiden, Markov stability, and METIS, 62,257 were feasible
and collapsed to 4,935 unique connected, size-bounded partitions.
Validation-only Pareto selection then produced 5 unique Cologne8 frontier
partitions and 16 unique Ingolstadt21 frontier partitions.
No optimizer dominated the front.
The practical lesson is simple: InfoMap is the natural flow-retaining
instrument for the hypothesis, while the final deployed partition should be
chosen by the retention criterion under the required size and connectivity
constraints.

Table~\ref{tab:causal} resolves the causal story for this regime.
The randomized intervention increases action entropy from roughly
$0.52$--$0.55$ to $0.79$ nats, so the design does perturb behavior, yet it
finds no held-out action-level coupling.
The mixed arm replaces each agent's action with a uniformly random valid

policy action.
The collection contains 400 mixed-arm and 100 single-agent intervention

The result is useful because it clarifies where the training gains should be
interpreted: not as evidence of preserved causal coupling across module
boundaries, but as consistent with
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{cond}}(\mathcal{P})$, the optimization-conditioning term
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:valueloss}.
This negative result also protects the story from overclaiming.
If the paper asserted that the training gains came from preserved causal
spill-back coupling, the perturbation result would contradict it.
Instead, the theory has a named place for partition-dependent learning
geometry, and the confirmed state-dependency retention is the signal that
makes this conditioning interpretation plausible.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The evidence supports the intended A--B--C chain rather than a loose
collection of metrics.
OD flow identifies where coordination pressure is concentrated; InfoMap
happens to penalize the stationary-walk escape term that complements
$\Phi_\pi$; empirical $\Phi_W$ then audits whether the same candidate retains
observed OD movement.
The resulting partitions retain more state-MI dependency than topology-only
METIS in every matched comparison.
The single-seed training case study is then read in that light: on
Ingolstadt21, the same Flow-over-METIS ordering gives 33.2\%--75.0\% lower
waiting time for IPPO and MAPPO.
Those runs are not a population-level RL performance claim, but they show
that the retained-dependency ordering remains meaningful when a learner
uses the partition.

The causal diagnostics sharpen the mechanism.
Spill-back is nearly absent, observational CMI finds no significant
action-to-queue edge family, and randomized perturbation finds no held-out
FDR-significant coupling after the action assignment is made exogenous.
The paper therefore does not claim that the reported training gains come
from preserved causal spill-back coupling.
We treat the conditioning term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:valueloss} as the leading
working explanation: flow-coherent regions group correlated reward and state
signals, which can reduce reward noise and make regional critics smaller and
better matched to the dominant dependencies.
The CoLight diagnostic supplies the boundary condition.
When attention is restricted to modules that are too small for one-hop
influence, compact topological neighborhoods can dominate flow corridors.

For deployment, the message is concrete.
Estimate route flow from representative trips, generate feasible candidate
partitions, score them by $\Phi$, $\Psi$, and connected-null
$\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}$ on validation traces, and train only the small
validation front.
This turns partition choice from a hidden modeling assumption into an
auditable pre-training design step.
The current limits are also clear: the transfer proof is stylized, the OD
matrix is measured before learned control reshapes traffic, the CoLight
result needs checkpoint replication, and the training case study needs the
usual multi-seed expansion.
A higher-demand arm is the decisive next test because it should activate
spill-back and reveal whether the same retention criterion becomes causal
rather than only conditioning.

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper argues that the right question for regional MARL in traffic
signal control is not which partitioner to prefer in the abstract, but what
the partition should retain.
The answer is vehicle-flow retention.
InfoMap is useful here because its map-equation objective favors
low stationary-walk escape, which makes it a natural candidate generator for
the retained-flow structure that the hypothesis asks to audit.
The experiments show that the generated flow partitions retain
15.4--32.5 percentage points more empirical route flow and 2.7--34.4
percentage points more state dependency than same-$k$ METIS partitions
across every tested network, module count, and state-MI seed.

The causal diagnostics make the claim sharper.
In these demand regimes, spill-back events are too sparse and randomized
action perturbations reveal no held-out action-level coupling.
The observed training gains are therefore consistent with optimization
conditioning: flow-coherent regions group correlated signals, reduce noisy
regional reward averaging, and make regional critics smaller and better
matched to the dominant dependencies.
The L3 CoLight diagnostic adds the main boundary condition: once modules are
too small for one-hop attention dependencies, compact neighborhood retention
can dominate flow retention.

The practical procedure is correspondingly direct.
Build an OD-induced route-flow graph from trips, generate candidate
partitions under the deployment's module-count, size, and connectivity
constraints, score them by $\Phi$, $\Psi$, and connected-null
$\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}$ on validation data, then policy-test a small
validation-front subset.
The criterion is the durable contribution.
The next experiments are a multi-seed training expansion and a higher-demand
regime where the causal coupling term has room to appear.

\section{Proof and Evidence Notes}
\label{app:proofs}

The proof stack supports three claims and no stronger one.
The flow link is exact for the stationary walk used by InfoMap.
The influence link is a stylized corridor-exposure bound.
The value link is imported from networked-MARL locality and controls the
structural loss from cutting influence.
MI, CMI, and Jacobians are empirical instruments for auditing the links; they
are not additional theoretical assumptions.

\noindent\textbf{Flow link.}
Let
$\mu^W_{ij}=W_{ij}/\sum_{a,b}W_{ab}$ be the empirical OD edge measure and
$\mu^\pi_{ij}=\pi_iP_{ij}$ be the stationary edge measure of the walk used
by InfoMap.
Then
$\Phi_W(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{c(i)=c(j)}\mu^W_{ij}$, while
$\Phi_\pi(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{c(i)=c(j)}\mu^\pi_{ij}$.
The map equation counts inter-module walk steps, so
\begin{equation}
    q_{\circlearrowleft}
    =
    \sum_{c(i)\neq c(j)} \mu^\pi_{ij}
    =
    1-\Phi_\pi(\mathcal{P}).
    \label{eq:app-stationary}
\end{equation}
This identity is exact for the stationary walk.
The empirical OD score coincides with it only when
$\mu^W=\mu^\pi$; otherwise
$|\,\Phi_\pi-\Phi_W\,|=\epsilon_{\mathrm{walk}}$ and
$1-\Phi_W\leq1-\Phi_\pi+\epsilon_{\mathrm{walk}}$.
This is why InfoMap is used as a flow-retaining candidate generator, while
the paper still audits $\Phi_W$ directly on observed trips.

\noindent\textbf{Influence link.}
The transfer statement is first a direct influence-retention bound.
Write $\Gamma_{ij}:=C_{j\leftarrow i}$ and assume
$\Gamma_{ij}\leq\alpha\widetilde{W}_{ij}+\beta_{ij}$, where
$\widetilde{W}_{ij}=W_{ij}+W_{ji}$ is bidirectional corridor-flow exposure.
Summing this pointwise bound over cut pairs gives
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{c(i)\neq c(j)}\Gamma_{ij}
    \leq
    \bar{\kappa}\sum_{c(i)\neq c(j)}\widetilde{W}_{ij}
    +\bar{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{dem}},
    \label{eq:app-cutinfl}
\end{equation}
where the residual collects the summed $\beta_{ij}$ slack, common-demand
terms, and model mismatch.
Dividing by total influence mass gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer}, with the
constants absorbed into $\kappa$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{dem}}$.
A stylized linear-Gaussian link-queue model provides an optional bridge from
this influence object to MI audits: in
$x_{t+1}=A x_t+B a_t+\eta_t$, stable covariance propagation charges any
cross-module covariance path to at least one cut corridor, and bounded
variances give a local Gaussian MI envelope.
Reverse OD flow is not treated as literal downstream blocking in this proof;
directed spill-back would require a separate blocked-sending state or storage
term.
Thus the result justifies the empirical question under a corridor-exposure
surrogate, not a full nonlinear spill-back theorem.

\noindent\textbf{Value link.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:valueloss} imports value-locality and
influence-abstraction results for networked MARL and interprets
$1-\Psi_\Gamma$ as the influence mass discarded by regionalization.
Those results bound structural bias from cutting influence; they do not
claim that the chosen learner optimizes equally well under every partition.
The optimization-conditioning term is therefore left explicit because
partitioning also changes gradient variance, critic dimension, and attention
reach.
That separation is what lets the paper connect positive state-dependency
retention with null causal probes without overstating either result.

\section{Diagnostic and Implementation Notes}
\label{app:diagnostics}

The simple random partition in the headline tables is a fixed floor, not a
statistical null distribution.
The benchmark uses exact-size and connectivity-preserving nulls with the same
module-size sequence; the connected null is the conservative deployment
control because it compares against other physically operable regions.

The instruments are deliberately separated.
$D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ is the main retention instrument because it is
training-free, replicated over three traffic seeds, and aligned with L1
regional rewards and the available L2 critic case study.
$D^G$ is computed on a global no-partition CoLight checkpoint so that
cross-module zeros are not imposed before measurement; with one checkpoint
per network, its $k=5$ reversal is an attention-boundary diagnostic.
Observed CMI and randomized perturbation are stricter causal probes.
Their null result means unresolved action-level coupling in this demand
regime, not global independence.

All regional learners consume the same stored partition assignment.
IPPO replaces each reward with the module mean, MAPPO gives each critic the
padded module observation, and CoLight changes the attention graph.
Thus IPPO/MAPPO form the clean L1/L2 case study, while CoLight is retained as
the L3 boundary condition.
