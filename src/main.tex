\begin{abstract}
    Coordinating traffic-signal agents in multi-agent reinforcement learning
    (MARL) requires partitioning intersections into coordination modules
    before training, yet existing practice inherits partitions from
    topology-based tools without a principled account of \emph{what a
        partition should preserve, and why}.
    We supply that account as a retention-based theory of partition quality,
    a three-link chain: (i)~the map equation's inter-module rate equals one
    minus the flow retention $\Phi$ \emph{exactly}, so InfoMap maximises an
    entropy-regularised flow retention; (ii)~under flow-local traffic
    dynamics, the flow a partition cuts upper-bounds the inter-agent
    dependency it cuts --- proved with explicit constants in a stylised
    link-queue model; and (iii)~under networked-MARL locality conditions, cut
    dependency upper-bounds the value loss of regional decomposition.
    We operationalise the theory with the dependency retention ratio $\Psi$,
    size-matched and connectivity-preserving null models, and a constrained,
    held-out community-detection benchmark.
    The confirmatory core: across two SUMO networks, three seeds, and every
    matched module count, flow-based partitions retain more flow and more
    state mutual information than METIS partitions --- 12 of 12 comparisons,
    the ordering the theory predicts.
    A preregistered randomized intervention then finds no resolvable
    action-level coupling in the tested demand regime; we therefore attribute
    the accompanying single-seed training gains under regional reward sharing
    and regional critics to \emph{optimisation conditioning} by correlated
    within-module signals, and state this evidentiary split explicitly.
    The benchmark bounds the claim's scope --- no single partitioning
    algorithm dominates under operational feasibility constraints --- so the
    durable contribution is the criterion and its evaluation frame:
    \emph{what a partition retains, not which optimiser produces it, is the
        load-bearing design choice}.

Urban traffic signal control (TSC) is a canonical large-scale coordination
problem: a phase decision at one intersection reshapes arrival patterns and
queue spill-back at its neighbours, so signals must be controlled jointly ---
yet the joint action space grows exponentially with the number of
intersections.
Practical multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) systems for TSC therefore
decompose the network into modules and coordinate \emph{within} them, whether
by shared rewards, centralised critics, communication, or
hierarchy~\citep{ma2020fma2c,wei2019colight,xu2021hilight,zhu2026halo}.
Every such system thus contains a \emph{coordination graph} that is fixed
before training: a decision about which agents share coordination machinery.
Unlike domains where physics dictates the interaction structure, in TSC this
graph is a free design variable (Section~\ref{sec:ndpomdp}) --- and its
design has received far less attention than the learning algorithms that run
on top of it.

When the coordination graph is designed at all, it is typically inherited
from general-purpose partitioners applied to road \emph{topology}: balanced
edge cut (METIS) in feudal MARL~\citep{ma2020fma2c,ma2022feudal}, spectral
partitioning of the road graph~\citep{laplacian2009ieee}, or clustering on
geometric and congestion features~\citep{communitydetection2021plosone,liu2023gplight}.
Flow-aware partitioning of urban networks does exist in the transport
literature --- origin--destination (OD) trace
partitioning~\citep{odflow2019partition} and MFD-based
zoning~\citep{mfdpartition2020} --- but it serves zoning and macroscopic
modelling, and is not connected to what \emph{learning agents} require of a
partition.
At the other extreme, attention-based MARL~\citep{jiang2018atoc,wei2019colight}
learns interaction structure implicitly, but gives no account of which
dependencies are preserved or severed when attention is restricted to
modules.
The gap that spans both lines of work is the rationale itself: \emph{no prior
    work supplies a principled account --- connecting network structure,
    information theory, and MARL performance --- of which partitioning criterion
    to choose and why}.
Partition choice is consequential, routinely made, and, in current practice,
unjustified.

This paper supplies that account by changing the question from \emph{which
    partitioner} to \emph{what a partition should preserve}.
Coordination pressure in traffic networks is carried by vehicle flow through
two physical channels --- forward, as routed platoons couple downstream
queues to upstream decisions, and backward, as spill-back throttles upstream
senders near saturation~\citep{daganzo1994ctm,jin2012linkqueue}.
The OD flow graph is therefore the natural substrate for partitioning, and
the map equation~\citep{rosvall2008maps} gives it an exact
information-theoretic objective.
Our theoretical contribution is a three-link \emph{retention chain}: the map
equation's inter-module rate equals one minus the flow retention $\Phi$
exactly (Proposition~\ref{prop:phi}); under flow-local dynamics, cut flow
upper-bounds cut inter-agent dependency, proved with explicit constants in a
stylised link-queue model (Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer});
and cut dependency upper-bounds the value loss of regional decomposition
under networked-MARL locality conditions (Theorem~\ref{thm:valueloss}).
Minimising the map equation therefore minimises an upper bound on the value
loss of regional MARL --- making flow retention a principled,
\emph{training-free} surrogate for coordination quality.
We measure the chain with a dependency retention ratio $\Psi$ instantiated by
three instruments (model-free mutual information, critic Jacobians, attention
Jacobians), across a coordination coupling spectrum that holds the partition
fixed while varying the mechanism: regional reward sharing (IPPO), a regional
centralised critic (MAPPO), and regional graph attention (CoLight)
(Section~\ref{sec:levels}).

Our contributions, with the evidentiary status of each stated explicitly:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A retention-based theory of partition quality}
          (Section~\ref{sec:method}): the first formal chain connecting
          map-equation minimisation to an upper bound on regional-MARL value
          loss, with an error decomposition that separates what a partition
          \emph{severs} (the coupling term) from how it \emph{conditions}
          optimisation (the $\epsilon_{\mathrm{cond}}$ term of
          Theorem~\ref{thm:valueloss}) --- the distinction our experiments
          turn out to need.
    \item \textbf{Standardised retention metrics and nulls}
          (Section~\ref{sec:psi}): $\Phi$, $\Psi$, size-adjusted
          $\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}$, exact-size and connectivity-preserving null
          families, and a partition-level permutation test that replaces
          underpowered pairwise gates.
    \item \textbf{Confirmatory retention evidence}
          (Sections~\ref{sec:exp-phi}--\ref{sec:exp-corr}): flow-based
          partitions retain more flow and more state mutual information than
          matched METIS partitions in all 12 same-$k$ comparisons across two
          SUMO networks and three seeds --- the ordering the theory predicts.
    \item \textbf{A mechanism finding, from a preregistered randomized
              intervention} (Section~\ref{sec:exp-perturb}): action-level causal
          coupling is not resolvable in the tested demand regime, so we
          attribute the accompanying single-seed training gains
          (Section~\ref{sec:exp-perf}) to \emph{optimisation conditioning} by
          correlated within-module signals --- and report the training result
          as a descriptive case study, not a powered comparison.
          A CoLight case study locates the theory's predicted module-size
          phase boundary rather than contradicting it
          (Section~\ref{sec:attnrange}).
    \item \textbf{A constrained, held-out community-detection benchmark}
          (Section~\ref{sec:exp-benchmark}): six algorithms on five
          dependency graphs under operational feasibility constraints, with
          validation-only Pareto selection.
          No optimiser dominates; unconstrained InfoMap partitions can be
          operationally infeasible; the choice of dependency graph, not
          optimiser, is load-bearing.
          The durable contribution is accordingly the \emph{criterion and its
              evaluation frame}, which any future partitioning proposal can be
          scored under --- not InfoMap as an algorithm.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Network-Distributed POMDP and the Coordination Graph}
\label{sec:ndpomdp}

Each signalised intersection $i \in \mathcal{V}$ observes a local state
$o_i$ (queue lengths, current phase, elapsed phase time) and selects a
phase action $a_i \in \mathcal{A}_i$.
We model the joint problem as a \emph{network-distributed POMDP} (ND-POMDP),
in which a coordination graph $\mathcal{G}_C = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}_C)$
encodes which agent pairs share explicit interaction terms in the joint reward.
Three structural properties of TSC motivate this formulation.

\textbf{(a) Reward locality with spill-back coupling.}
The immediate reward $r_i$ depends primarily on queues and delays at
intersection $i$, making it nearly local.
However, when downstream lanes saturate, backward-propagating queue waves
couple $r_i$ to the actions at neighbouring intersection $j$ via
spill-back~\citep{dependencydyn2025}.
The ND-POMDP captures this through interaction terms:
\begin{equation}
    R(\mathbf{a})
    \;=\; \sum_{i \in \mathcal{V}} r_i(o_i, a_i)
    \;+\; \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_C} r_{ij}(o_i, o_j, a_i, a_j).
    \label{eq:ndpomdp}
\end{equation}
Edges in $\mathcal{G}_C$ mark pairs where the cross-agent term $r_{ij}$ is
non-negligible; omitting a spill-back edge misattributes joint outcomes to
independent agents.

\textbf{(b) Tractability via maximum clique size.}
Exact coordination over a clique of $\omega$ agents requires evaluating
$|\mathcal{A}|^\omega$ joint actions.
Partitioning $\mathcal{V}$ into modules of bounded size
$|M_k| \leq \omega_{\max}$ keeps this tractable; in TSC networks
$\omega_{\max} = 4$--$6$ is sufficient in practice.

\textbf{(c) $\mathcal{G}_C$ is a free design variable.}
Unlike environments where the coordination graph is determined by the physics,
in TSC the coordination modules are explicitly constructed before training.
This makes partition selection the central algorithmic question: which graph
minimises the coordination cost severed by module boundaries?
Attribute~(a) answers what ``coordination cost'' means: the mass of spill-back
interaction terms $r_{ij}$ cut by the partition.
Attributes~(a)--(c) together motivate using OD vehicle flow as the partition
criterion, and Section~\ref{sec:infomap} makes this precise.

\subsection{The Map Equation}
\label{sec:mapeq}

InfoMap~\citep{rosvall2008maps,rosvall2009mapequation} finds a partition by
minimising the description length of a random walk on a weighted directed graph:
\begin{equation}
    L(\mathcal{M})
    \;=\; q_{\circlearrowleft} H(\mathcal{Q})
    \;+\; \sum_{k=1}^{K} p_k^{\circlearrowleft} H(\mathcal{P}_k),
    \label{eq:mapeq}
\end{equation}
where $q_{\circlearrowleft}$ is the aggregate rate of inter-module random-walk
transitions, $H(\mathcal{Q})$ is the entropy of the between-module codebook,
$p_k^{\circlearrowleft}$ is the within-module random-walk rate for $M_k$, and
$H(\mathcal{P}_k)$ is the within-module codebook entropy.
Minimising $L(\mathcal{M})$ minimises the average bits required to describe
the walker's trajectory, which is achieved by grouping nodes that exchange the
most flow into the same module --- so that the walker rarely crosses module
boundaries and the between-module term $q_{\circlearrowleft}H(\mathcal{Q})$
stays small.

Applied to $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{OD}}$, InfoMap groups intersections that
exchange the most vehicle volume.
As we show in Section~\ref{sec:theory}, these are exactly the pairs most
likely to be coordination-necessary via spill-back, connecting the
information-theoretic objective of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mapeq} to the traffic-theoretic
requirement of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ndpomdp}.

The map equation belongs to the family of \emph{flow-based} partition
objectives, which evaluate a partition by the behaviour of a dynamical process
on the network, in contrast to \emph{combinatorial} objectives such as
minimum cut or balanced edge cut (METIS) that count edges irrespective of the
flow they carry.
Markov stability~\citep{delvenne2010stability,lambiotte2014multiscale} makes
this distinction canonical: flow-based objectives reward partitions in which a
random walker remains in the same module across consecutive steps, so a
flow-based and a combinatorial partition of the same network are categorically
different objects even when they look superficially similar.
Unlike modularity maximisation~\citep{newmangirvan2004}, the map equation does
not inherit modularity's resolution limit, although it is not scale-free
either: it has known field-of-view limitations and can over-partition
constrained, grid-like networks~\citep{edler2023community}.
Infomap itself is a greedy stochastic search over an NP-hard landscape and
does not guarantee the global codelength optimum~\citep{edler2023community}.

\paragraph{Network partitioning for traffic control.}
Partitioning is already standard machinery in large-scale TSC, but the
criterion is almost always structural or geometric: spectral partitioning of
the road graph~\citep{laplacian2009ieee}, balanced edge cut via METIS
inherited by feudal MARL~\citep{ma2020fma2c,ma2022feudal} and region-based
hierarchies~\citep{regionlight2024,xu2021hilight}, congestion-feature
clustering~\citep{communitydetection2021plosone}, and dynamic grouping by
learned similarity~\citep{liu2023gplight}.
Where flow information does enter, it does so for transport-planning ends:
OD-trace partitioning for zoning~\citep{odflow2019partition} and MFD-based
partitioning for perimeter control~\citep{mfdpartition2020} identify
flow-coherent regions, but neither connects the partition to the coordination
cost incurred by learning agents --- the partition is an end product, not a
coordination-graph design with a downstream learner whose value loss it
controls.
Our gap claim is therefore deliberately narrow: not that flow-based
partitioning is new to traffic science, but that no prior work provides a
\emph{rationale} --- of any formal kind --- for which criterion a
coordination-graph designer should optimise, or an instrument for auditing
what a chosen partition severs.

\paragraph{Coordination topology in MARL.}
A complementary literature learns or fixes the interaction structure inside
the learner: attentional communication~\citep{jiang2018atoc}, individually
inferred communication~\citep{i2c2021}, structured and correlated
communication topologies~\citep{peng2020structured,aamas2021correlated},
neighbourhood communication in networked
control~\citep{neurcomm}, graph attention over road
topology~\citep{wei2019colight}, information-bottleneck coordination
graphs~\citep{hetib2025,sun2026ibvq,wang2020imac,yu2023lowentropymarl}, and
scalability-oriented topologies~\citep{expocomm2025,li2025topology,zhu2026halo}.
These methods discover useful structure implicitly, but provide no external
account of which dependencies the learned or imposed topology preserves ---
the structure lives inside the model and cannot be audited before training.
Our approach is complementary rather than competing: we fix the partition
from a training-free criterion and use the trained models' own dependency
structure (Section~\ref{sec:psi}) to evaluate the partition after the fact.

\paragraph{Measuring inter-agent dependency.}
Our instruments adapt existing measurement frameworks to a new question.
Spatio-temporal mutual information between intersections is established for
TSC~\citep{stmarl2020}, and its mechanistic link to spill-back has been made
explicit~\citep{dependencydyn2025}; we use it as the model-free dependency
weight $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$.
Jacobian-based influence profiles diagnose over-squashing in
GNNs~\citep{lrd2025}, with antecedents in attention-flow and attribution
analysis~\citep{abnar2020rollout,simonyan2013saliency,sundararajan2017axiomatic};
we repurpose the same instrument to ask whether a \emph{proposed partition}
aligns with what a trained model depends on ($D^{Q}$, $D^{G}$) --- a distinct
scientific question from architectural diagnosis.
The retention ratio $\Psi$ then aggregates any such matrix into a
partition-level score with size-matched and connectivity-preserving nulls,
which we did not find elsewhere in the MARL literature.

\paragraph{Theory imports.}
The retention chain connects three literatures that have not previously met.
From network science: the map
equation~\citep{rosvall2008maps,rosvall2009mapequation,edler2023community}
and the flow-based reading of community detection given by Markov
stability~\citep{delvenne2010stability,lambiotte2014multiscale}, including
its memory~\citep{rosvall2014memory} and dynamic~\citep{flowstability2022}
refinements.
From networked MARL: exponential-decay value-locality
bounds~\citep{qu2020average,qu2022scalable}, their spectral
weakening~\citep{chakraborty2026locality}, and influence-based-abstraction
loss bounds~\citep{congeduti2021iba}, in the ND-POMDP
formalism~\citep{nair2005ndpomdp,kok2006ndpomdp,oliehoek2012ndpomdp}.
From traffic flow theory: cell-transmission and link-queue
dynamics~\citep{daganzo1994ctm,jin2012linkqueue} and the Lyapunov-drift
stability tradition behind max-pressure
control~\citep{tassiulas1992stability,varaiya2013maxpressure}.
None of these alone links map-equation minimisation to regional-MARL value
loss; the chain of Section~\ref{sec:method} is that connection.

\paragraph{Community-detection baselines.}
The benchmark of Section~\ref{sec:exp-benchmark} draws its candidate
algorithms from the standard repertoire: normalised spectral
cut~\citep{shimalik2000}, Leiden under the constant Potts
model~\citep{traag2019leiden,traag2011cpm}, Markov
stability~\citep{delvenne2010stability,lambiotte2014multiscale},
METIS~\citep{karypis1998metis}, and Infomap~\citep{rosvall2008maps}.
We claim no new detector; the benchmark's contribution is the harness ---
operational feasibility constraints, null families, and held-out selection
--- under which we find that no detector dominates
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-benchmark}).

\section{Flow-Coherent Dependency Partitioning}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{OD Flow Graph Construction}
\label{sec:odgraph}

We model the traffic network as a directed weighted graph
$\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{OD}} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}, \mathbf{W})$,
where $\mathcal{V}$ is the set of $N$ signalised intersections and $W_{ij} \geq 0$
is the observed vehicle count travelling from intersection $i$ to intersection $j$
over a representative time window.
We construct $\mathbf{W}$ from simulation trip logs: for each completed vehicle
trip, the sequence of intersections visited is recorded and the corresponding
directed edge weights incremented.
A warm-up period of approximately one hour under a uniformly random policy provides
a flow snapshot that reflects network demand structure prior to any learned behaviour.

The key inductive bias is that $\mathbf{W}$ captures \emph{demand} rather than
\emph{road topology}: two intersections connected by a high-volume corridor appear
as a strong edge regardless of their hop distance on the road graph.
This is what separates OD-flow partitioning from METIS on the adjacency matrix
and from Laplacian spectral methods~\citep{laplacian2009ieee}: both of the latter
assign edge weight by physical connectivity, not by how many vehicles actually
travel between node pairs.

\subsection{InfoMap Partitioning and Flow Retention}
\label{sec:infomap}
\label{sec:theory}

Given $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{OD}}$, we apply the Infomap
algorithm~\citep{rosvall2008maps,rosvall2009mapequation} to obtain a partition
$\mathcal{M} = \{M_1, \ldots, M_K\}$ by minimising the map equation of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mapeq}.
Our theoretical account of \emph{why} this is the right objective is a
three-link chain, developed in this and the following two subsections:
the map equation is an exact statement about the flow a partition retains
(Proposition~\ref{prop:phi}); under flow-local traffic dynamics, retained flow
bounds retained inter-agent dependency (Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer});
and retained dependency bounds the value loss of regional MARL
(Theorem~\ref{thm:valueloss}).
The empirical program of Section~\ref{sec:experiments} then estimates the
constants of these bounds rather than gating on binary hypothesis tests.

\paragraph{Flow retention.}
For a partition $\mathcal{P} = \{P_1, \ldots, P_K\}$ with module assignment
$c(\cdot)$ and OD flow matrix $\mathbf{W}$, define the \emph{flow retention
    ratio}
\begin{equation}
    \Phi(\mathcal{P}, \mathbf{W})
    = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k}\sum_{i,j \,\in\, P_k} W_{ij}}
    {\displaystyle\sum_{i,j} W_{ij}},
    \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
the fraction of observed vehicle movements that stay within module boundaries.

\begin{proposition}[Flow retention is the map equation's inter-module rate]
    \label{prop:phi}
    Let the random walk in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mapeq} be the empirical OD walk:
    visit rates $p_i = \sum_j W_{ij} / \sum_{a,b} W_{ab}$ and transition
    probabilities $p_{ij} = W_{ij} / \sum_{j'} W_{ij'}$, so that the stationary
    edge measure is $p_i\, p_{ij} = W_{ij} / \sum_{a,b} W_{ab}$.
    Then the aggregate inter-module transition rate in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mapeq}
    satisfies
    \begin{equation}
        q_{\circlearrowleft}
        \;=\; \sum_{k} \sum_{i \in P_k}\sum_{j \notin P_k} p_i\, p_{ij}
        \;=\; \frac{\sum_{i,j:\, c(i) \neq c(j)} W_{ij}}{\sum_{i,j} W_{ij}}
        \;=\; 1 - \Phi(\mathcal{P}, \mathbf{W}).
        \label{eq:qphi}
    \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    Substitute the empirical edge measure into the definition of
    $q_{\circlearrowleft}$ as the total stationary probability of inter-module
    steps~\citep[cf.][]{edler2023community} and sum over all ordered pairs with
    $c(i) \neq c(j)$; the last equality is the complement of
    Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi}.
\end{proof}

$\Phi$ is therefore not a heuristic proxy loosely associated with the map
equation: it \emph{is} the map equation's between-module rate, exactly, under
the walk model induced by the observed OD flows.

\begin{corollary}[InfoMap maximises entropy-regularised flow retention]
    \label{cor:regularised}
    Writing Eq.~\eqref{eq:mapeq} as
    $L(\mathcal{M}) = (1 - \Phi)\, H(\mathcal{Q})
        + \sum_{k} p_k^{\circlearrowleft} H(\mathcal{P}_k)$,
    the map equation is, at any fixed module-usage profile (fixed codebook
    entropies and within-module rates), monotone increasing in $1 - \Phi$.
    Minimising $L(\mathcal{M})$ thus maximises an \emph{entropy-regularised}
    flow retention: the codebook-entropy terms are the regulariser that rules
    out the degenerate all-in-one-module solution, which attains $\Phi = 1$
    but pays the full within-module description cost.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Information-theoretic reading.}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:phi}, $1 - \Phi$ is the per-step probability that the
coarse-grained state --- the walker's module label $m(t)$ --- changes.
A low inter-module rate means the module-label sequence is highly compressible,
i.e.\ the coarse-grained dynamics are maximally predictable.
Up to the null-model term, this is the one-step clustered autocovariance that
Markov stability maximises~\citep{delvenne2010stability,lambiotte2014multiscale}:
the probability that a walker occupies the same community at $t$ and $t{+}1$.
Equivalently, a partition with a low exit rate makes the chain nearly lumpable,
so InfoMap can be read as finding the coarse-graining of the OD walk that
loses the least description length.
In the ND-POMDP interpretation of Section~\ref{sec:ndpomdp}, minimising
cross-module walk rate is minimising cross-module coordination overhead while
maximising within-module coordination coherence.

\paragraph{What the empirical $\Phi$ comparison adds.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:phi} makes
$\Phi(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{InfoMap}}) \geq \Phi(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{METIS}})$
at matched module count a theorem-shaped expectation rather than an empirical
surprise.
Measuring it on real OD data (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}) remains
informative for two reasons.
First, real trips are path-dependent, so the memoryless-walk model behind
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mapeq} approximates actual routing; the higher-order map
equation is the standard refinement~\citep{rosvall2014memory}.
Second, Infomap's greedy stochastic search does not guarantee the global
codelength optimum~\citep{edler2023community}.
Third, directed Infomap regularises the walk with teleportation, so the visit
rates it actually optimises differ slightly from the empirical OD rates under
which Proposition~\ref{prop:phi} is exact.
The observed margins quantify how much of the identity survives these gaps.

\subsection{Spatial Dependency Instruments and $\Psi$}
\label{sec:psi}

To measure how much dependency a partition retains, we need per-pair dependency
weights $D_{ij}$ that (i)~are computable without presupposing the partition,
(ii)~yield a scalar per pair $(i,j)$, and (iii)~can be aggregated into a
partition-level score. We introduce three complementary instruments, each matched
to the coordination mechanism at a given coupling level.

\paragraph{Physical dependency (model-free).}
The mutual information between agent state observations supplies a model-free
dependency weight:
\begin{equation}
    D^{\mathrm{MI}}_{ij}
    = \hat{I}\!\left(X_i(t);\, X_j(t{+}\tau)\right)
    \label{eq:dmi}
\end{equation}
estimated by the KSG $k$-nearest-neighbour estimator~\citep{kraskov2004ksg}
from warm-up trajectories.
$D^{\mathrm{MI}}_{ij}$ is model-free and applicable at L1: at the reward
coupling level (IPPO/IDQN), agents act independently and there is no
cross-agent input path in the policy network, making Jacobian-based measures
structurally inapplicable.
Spatial MI dominance in TSC networks is established by~\citet{stmarl2020},
and its mechanistic link to spill-back is established by~\citet{dependencydyn2025},
grounding $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ as the natural physical dependency measure.

\paragraph{Learned dependency (Jacobian on the global model).}
For models that receive cross-agent inputs, we adapt the Jacobian influence
framework of~\citet{lrd2025} to the MARL dependency measurement setting.
For a model $F$ trained \emph{without} partition constraints --- the global model
--- define the spatial influence of agent~$j$ on agent~$i$:
\begin{equation}
    D_{i\leftarrow j}(F)
    = \sum_{a,k}
    \left|\frac{\partial F_{i,a}}{\partial X_{j,k}}\right|
    \label{eq:djac}
\end{equation}
where $X_{j,k}$ is feature~$k$ of agent~$j$'s observation and $F_{i,a}$ is
output dimension~$a$ at agent~$i$.
The \emph{global-model restriction} is essential: in a partitioned model,
cross-module inputs are absent by construction, making cross-module Jacobians
identically zero --- a structural artefact, not evidence of low dependency.
We instantiate Eq.~\eqref{eq:djac} at two levels:
\begin{align}
    D^{Q}_{i\leftarrow j}
     & = D_{i\leftarrow j}(V^{\mathrm{global}})
     &                                                         & \text{(global MAPPO critic, L2)}
    \label{eq:dq}                                                                                          \\
    D^{G}_{i\leftarrow j}
     & = D_{i\leftarrow j}(\mathbf{h}^{(L),\mathrm{global}}_i)
     &                                                         & \text{(global CoLight GAT embedding, L3)}
    \label{eq:dg}
\end{align}
Whereas \citet{lrd2025} use Jacobian profiles to ask whether a GNN suffers
from over-squashing, we use the same instrument to ask whether a proposed partition
aligns with what the model has learned to depend on --- a distinct scientific question.

\paragraph{Control dependency (conditional MI).}
The instrument the theory ultimately targets is the influence of agent $i$'s
\emph{action} on agent $j$'s future state,
\begin{equation}
    D^{\mathrm{CMI}}_{ij}
    = \hat{I}\!\left(a_i^{t};\, q_j^{t+\tau} \,\middle|\, q_j^{t},\, a_j^{t}\right),
    \label{eq:dcmi}
\end{equation}
estimated on discretised queue states with permutation bias correction
(within-episode circular action shifts) and Benjamini--Hochberg control
across directed pairs~\citep{benjamini1995fdr}.
Unlike $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$, which measures co-fluctuation,
$D^{\mathrm{CMI}}$ conditions away the target's own history and action, so a
significant edge is evidence of \emph{control} coupling --- the quantity that
distinguishes traffic similarity from traffic dependency from control
dependency.
We define the instrument here but flag its empirical status up front: no
$D^{\mathrm{CMI}}$ edge survives FDR correction on either network, either
observationally (Section~\ref{sec:exp-benchmark}) or under a preregistered
randomized action intervention that removes the observational identification
problem outright (Section~\ref{sec:exp-perturb}).
$D^{\mathrm{CMI}}$ and its interventional counterpart therefore enter our
experiments only as exploratory ranking signals, never as confirmed causal
structure.

\paragraph{Hop-distance profile.}
For either instrument, aggregate dependency by road-graph hop distance $\rho(i,j)$:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{D}_h
    = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}|}\sum_{i}\sum_{j:\,\rho(i,j)=h} D_{i\leftarrow j}
    \label{eq:barDh}
\end{equation}
This is the MARL analogue of the per-hop influence profile $\bar{T}_h$
of~\citet{lrd2025}: $\bar{D}_h$ quantifies the average spatial dependency
from agents exactly $h$ road-graph hops away, and its decay rate determines
the minimum module radius needed for effective coordination.

\paragraph{Dependency retention ratio $\Psi$.}
Given dependency matrix $\mathbf{D}$ and partition $\mathcal{P}=\{P_1,\ldots,P_K\}$:
\begin{equation}
    \Psi(\mathcal{P},\,\mathbf{D})
    = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k}\sum_{i,j\,\in\, P_k} D_{ij}}
    {\displaystyle\sum_{i,j} D_{ij}}
    \label{eq:psi}
\end{equation}
$\Psi \in [0,1]$ is the \emph{dependency retention ratio}: the fraction of total
inter-agent dependency captured within module boundaries.
High $\Psi$ means the partition cuts predominantly low-dependency edges ---
preserving coordination capacity --- while low $\Psi$ means high-dependency edges
are severed, imposing coordination cost.

\paragraph{Relation to existing partition quality metrics.}
Graph modularity $Q$~\citep{newmangirvan2004} and normalised
cut~\citep{shimalik2000} are the standard partition quality metrics.
$\Psi$ differs from both in two respects.
First, $\Psi$ requires no null model: $\mathbf{D}$ is already a semantically
meaningful quantity (MI or Jacobian sensitivity), not raw structural adjacency
for which a random-graph null is appropriate.
Second, $\mathbf{D}$ is asymmetric and non-negative (direction matters: agent $j$
influences $i$ via traffic flow), whereas $Q$ and normalised cut assume undirected
graphs.
By computing $\Psi(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{InfoMap}},\mathbf{D})$ vs.\
$\Psi(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{METIS}},\mathbf{D})$ on the \emph{same} $\mathbf{D}$,
we obtain a direct, architecture-independent measure of which partition better
preserves the inter-agent dependency structure.

\paragraph{Size-adjusted retention and partition-level significance.}
Raw $\Psi$ is biased toward coarse partitions: larger modules retain more of
any $\mathbf{D}$ simply by cutting fewer pairs.
We therefore report, alongside $\Psi$, the chance-adjusted score
\begin{equation}
    \Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}(\mathcal{P}, \mathbf{D})
    = \frac{\Psi(\mathcal{P}, \mathbf{D})
        - \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}' \sim \mathcal{R}(\mathcal{P})}
        \!\left[\Psi(\mathcal{P}', \mathbf{D})\right]}
    {1 - \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}' \sim \mathcal{R}(\mathcal{P})}
        \!\left[\Psi(\mathcal{P}', \mathbf{D})\right]},
    \label{eq:psiadj}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{P})$ draws random partitions with the same
module-size sequence as $\mathcal{P}$.
$\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}$ measures the excess dependency retained beyond
size-matched chance, in the spirit of chance-adjusted clustering-comparison
scores, and makes partitions with different module-size profiles comparable.
Because operational partitions are further constrained to spatially connected
modules, we also use a \emph{connected} null: random partitions with the same
module-size sequence \emph{and} physically connected modules, drawn by seeded
random growth on the road graph.
Spatial connectivity alone retains dependency above unrestricted chance, so
the connected null is the conservative reference, and it is the one the
benchmark of Section~\ref{sec:exp-benchmark} adopts for
$\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}$.
The same null supplies the partition-level significance test we use in place
of pairwise hypothesis tests: holding $\mathbf{D}$ fixed, sample size-matched
random partitions and report the percentile of the candidate partition's
within-module mass $\sum_{c(i)=c(j)} D_{ij}$ under this null.
This yields one well-powered statistic per (network, $K$, seed) instead of
hundreds of noisy pairwise estimates --- the test the theory of
Section~\ref{sec:transfer} actually licenses.

\subsection{From Flow to Dependency: A Transfer Lemma}
\label{sec:transfer}

The second link of the chain connects the flow a partition retains
(Proposition~\ref{prop:phi}) to the dependency it retains
(Section~\ref{sec:psi}).
The claim is a stylised-model bound, stated under explicit assumptions on the
traffic dynamics; it is a property of flow-local traffic networks, not of
arbitrary multi-agent systems.

\begin{assumption}[Flow-local dynamics]
    \label{ass:local}
    The traffic state evolves as a Markov chain whose transition kernel
    factorises over the road graph: $X_i(t{+}1)$ depends only on
    $\{X_j(t) : j \in \mathcal{N}(i) \cup \{i\}\}$ and exogenous demand
    $\xi(t)$, as in cell-transmission and link-queue
    models~\citep{daganzo1994ctm,jin2012linkqueue}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Flow-bounded coupling]
    \label{ass:coupling}
    One-step inter-intersection coupling is bounded proportionally to the flow
    exchanged: there exists a constant $\alpha$ (a function of link capacities
    and saturation rates) such that the Dobrushin-style influence coefficient
    obeys
    $C_{j \leftarrow i} \leq \alpha \, (W_{ij} + W_{ji}) + \beta_{ij}$,
    where $\beta_{ij}$ collects coupling through common exogenous demand.
    The two flow terms are the two physical transmission channels of traffic:
    the \emph{forward (arrival) channel} --- downstream queue state depends on
    upstream state only through the vehicles actually routed between them ---
    and the \emph{backward (blocking) channel} --- finite link capacity
    throttles upstream senders in proportion to their sending flow when
    downstream space runs out~\citep{daganzo1994ctm,jin2012linkqueue}, i.e.\
    spill-back propagates on the reverse flow graph.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Regime stationarity]
    \label{ass:stationary}
    Demand statistics are stationary over the measurement window, so
    $\mathbf{W}$ and $\mathbf{D}$ are estimated within a single traffic regime.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Cut flow upper-bounds cut dependency]
    \label{prop:transfer}
    Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:local}--\ref{ass:stationary}, the dependency
    mass cut by a partition is bounded by the flow it cuts: there exist
    $\kappa > 0$ and a demand-confounder term
    $\epsilon_{\mathrm{dem}} \geq 0$ such that
    \begin{equation}
        1 - \Psi(\mathcal{P}, \mathbf{D})
        \;\leq\;
        \kappa \left(1 - \Phi(\mathcal{P}, \widetilde{\mathbf{W}})\right)
        + \epsilon_{\mathrm{dem}},
        \label{eq:transfer}
    \end{equation}
    where $\widetilde{W}_{ij} = W_{ij} + W_{ji}$ is the symmetrised flow,
    $\kappa$ absorbs $\alpha$ and the normalisations of $\mathbf{D}$ and
    $\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}$, and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{dem}}$ aggregates the
    common-demand terms $\beta_{ij}$ across the cut.
\end{proposition}

Appendix~\ref{app:proofs:transfer} proves the bound with explicit constants
in a stylised linear--Gaussian link-queue model in which
Assumptions~\ref{ass:local}--\ref{ass:stationary} hold literally
(Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer-linear}); in that model the demand confounder
can moreover be removed \emph{exactly} by conditioning on the demand process
(Corollary~\ref{cor:demzero}).
The general nonlinear case remains a conjecture, with the tools required for
it identified in the appendix.
The direction of the bound matters: low cut flow certifies low cut dependency,
but high $\Psi$ does not certify high performance --- the bound is necessary-,
not sufficient-, for lossless decomposition.
The constant $\kappa$ and the confounder $\epsilon_{\mathrm{dem}}$ are
network- and regime-dependent; the experiments estimate them rather than
assume them away.

\paragraph{The role of $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$: a one-sided witness.}
The chain can be read with \emph{influence}, not mutual information, as the
central object.
The value-loss results imported in Section~\ref{sec:valueloss} are stated in
terms of one-step influence coefficients --- exactly the quantity
Assumption~\ref{ass:coupling} bounds by flow --- so the theory track (flow
$\Rightarrow$ influence $\Rightarrow$ value loss) never needs MI at all.
MI enters on the \emph{measurement} track: by data processing, the
statistical dependency observable across a cut cannot exceed a function of
the influence crossing it, so large measured cut-MI \emph{certifies} large
cut influence, whereas small measured cut-MI does not certify small influence
--- a channel can be strong yet unexercised by the demand distribution in the
measurement window.
$\Psi(D^{\mathrm{MI}})$ comparisons therefore license ruling partitions
\emph{out} (a partition that cuts much MI provably cuts much influence, so
its value-loss bound is poor) rather than certifying winners --- the same
necessary-not-sufficient reading as Theorem~\ref{thm:valueloss}, now at the
level of the instrument itself.

\paragraph{H1 and H2 as operational signatures.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}'s two channels yield two testable empirical
signatures, which earlier iterations of this work treated as a mandatory
pre-training gate.
The theory clarifies their correct epistemic status: they are
\emph{corollaries in expectation} of the transfer bound --- diagnostics that
the mechanism is active --- not axioms the method depends on.
We report both as supporting evidence; failure on a given network localises
which channel is inactive there, rather than invalidating the partition.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{H1 (Spatial MI Advantage --- forward-channel signature).}
Within-module agent pairs share higher mutual information about future traffic
states than cross-module pairs:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}_{i,j\in M_k}\!\left[I(X_i(t);\, X_j(t{+}\tau))\right]
    >
    \mathbb{E}_{i\in M_k,\, l\notin M_k}\!\left[I(X_i(t);\, X_l(t{+}\tau))\right]
    \label{eq:h1}
\end{equation}
averaged over propagation lags $\tau \in \{1, 5, 15, 30\}$ steps, with MI
estimated by the KSG estimator~\citep{kraskov2004ksg} and visualised as a
$|\mathcal{V}|\times|\mathcal{V}|$ heatmap with module boundaries overlaid.
Note what the theory does and does not predict here:
Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer} bounds \emph{aggregate} cut dependency mass,
not the ordering of every individual pair $(i, j, l)$.
A uniform pairwise test --- e.g.\ a Wilcoxon signed-rank test over all pairs
--- therefore tests a strictly stronger claim than the theory makes, and
marginal pairwise pass rates are expected rather than anomalous.
The theory-matched test is the partition-level permutation test of
Section~\ref{sec:psi}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{H2 (Spill-back Containment --- backward-channel signature).}
Spill-back congestion is more likely to remain within InfoMap modules than to
cross module boundaries:
\begin{equation}
    \Pr\!\left(\text{spill-back}_{i\to j}
    \;\middle|\; i,j \in M_k\right)
    >
    \Pr\!\left(\text{spill-back}_{i\to l}
    \;\middle|\; i \in M_k,\; l \notin M_k\right),
    \label{eq:h2}
\end{equation}
assessed via a proportion test on SUMO queue trajectory data.
\citet{dependencydyn2025} establish that inter-agent coordination is necessary
\emph{if and only if} spill-back connects two agents' queues.
The apparent directional asymmetry --- OD flow travels $i \to j$ (forward)
while spill-back propagates $j \to i$ (backward) --- is precisely the
two-channel structure of Assumption~\ref{ass:coupling}: a high-volume corridor
$W_{ij}$ drives high arrival rates at $j$ which, under near-saturated demand,
trigger backward-propagating queue waves toward $i$~\citep{dependencydyn2025}.
The coordination-necessary pair and the high-OD-flow pair are thus the same
pair on the same corridor.
Crucially, the backward channel is active \emph{only near saturation}: in a
network or demand regime that never leaves free flow, spill-back events are
absent, the backward channel is inert, and all measured dependency is
arrival-driven.
An empty H2 test on such a network is a statement about the regime, not a
refutation of the mechanism.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Why containment, not just correlation, matters.}
Spill-back containment has a formal interpretation beyond the statistical test
above.
In the Lyapunov-drift framework for constrained queueing
networks~\citep{tassiulas1992stability}, a decentralised scheduler preserves
the stability region of a centralised one only if it can react to the joint
queue state of servers whose queues are dynamically coupled; max-pressure
signal control~\citep{varaiya2013maxpressure} instantiates exactly this drift
argument for signalised intersections via a quadratic Lyapunov function over
per-approach queue lengths.
Read against that framework, H2 states the condition under which cutting a
coordination-graph edge is safe: an edge can be severed without sacrificing
the stability that joint observation would provide only if spill-back across
it is rare.
We do not derive a stability guarantee for the learned MARL policies studied
here; we invoke this literature as the control-theoretic motivation for
treating spill-back rate, rather than mutual information alone, as the
quantity that determines which edges a partition may safely cut.

\paragraph{The demand confounder.}
The term $\epsilon_{\mathrm{dem}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer} is not a
formality.
Network-wide demand fluctuations act as a common cause, creating mutual
information between intersections that exchange \emph{no} flow, and this
common-cause mass grows with network size --- predicting that the raw
alignment between $\mathbf{W}$ and $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ weakens on larger
networks even when the flow-borne coupling is unchanged.
The theory-driven fix is to estimate $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ \emph{conditionally}
on an aggregate demand covariate (e.g.\ total network occupancy at time $t$),
which removes the common-cause channel by construction --- exactly so in the
stylised model (Corollary~\ref{cor:demzero}).
Model-derived instruments such as $D^{G}$ (Section~\ref{sec:psi}) largely do
not inherit the confounder: a trained model has no incentive to route
gradient through agents that carry no decision-relevant information.

\subsection{From Dependency Retention to Regional Value Loss}
\label{sec:valueloss}

The third link imports value-loss bounds from networked MARL and
influence-based abstraction, conditional on the coupling structure established
by Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}.
In networked MARL, when one-step influence is local, the sensitivity of one
agent's value to another decays exponentially with graph distance, and
policies truncated to an $r$-hop neighbourhood approximate the global optimum
with bias decaying exponentially in
$r$~\citep{qu2022scalable,qu2020average};
recent work replaces the classical row-sum (Dobrushin) condition for this
locality with a weaker, policy-dependent spectral certificate
$\rho(H^{\pi}) < 1$~\citep{chakraborty2026locality}.
Influence-based abstraction bounds the value loss of a local model by the
error of its representation of the influence the rest of the system exerts on
it~\citep{congeduti2021iba}.
A regional decomposition is exactly such a truncation: each module models
within-module influence and ignores the rest, so the discarded influence is
the cut dependency mass.

\begin{theorem}[Regional value loss is controlled by cut dependency; informal]
    \label{thm:valueloss}
    Let $J^{\star}$ denote optimal global coordination performance and
    $J_{\mathcal{P}}$ the performance of regional MARL under partition
    $\mathcal{P}$.
    Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:local}--\ref{ass:coupling} and either the
    value-locality conditions of \citet{qu2022scalable} or the
    approximate-influence conditions of \citet{congeduti2021iba}, there exists
    $C > 0$ such that
    \begin{equation}
        J^{\star} - J_{\mathcal{P}}
        \;\leq\;
        C \left(1 - \Psi(\mathcal{P}, \mathbf{D})\right)
        + \epsilon_{\mathrm{approx}}
        + \epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathcal{P}),
        \label{eq:valueloss}
    \end{equation}
    where $C > 0$ is uniform over partitions of a fixed network and demand
    regime (a $C$ allowed to vary with $\mathcal{P}$ would make the bound
    uninformative for comparisons), $\epsilon_{\mathrm{approx}}$ is
    function-approximation error, and the optimisation error realised under
    partition $\mathcal{P}$ decomposes as
    $\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathcal{P})
        = \epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}^{0} + \epsilon_{\mathrm{cond}}(\mathcal{P})$:
    a partition-independent training-noise floor, plus a
    partition-\emph{dependent} \emph{conditioning term} --- the partition's
    effect on the optimisation problem itself, through credit-assignment
    variance under regional reward averaging, critic input dimension, and
    replay coherence.
    Chaining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:qphi}, \eqref{eq:transfer}, and
    \eqref{eq:valueloss}: \emph{minimising the map equation minimises an upper
        bound on the value loss of regional decomposition in MARL}.
\end{theorem}

Derivation notes are given in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}; the theorem imports
its heavy machinery from \citet{qu2022scalable} and \citet{congeduti2021iba}
and our contribution is the construction that connects them to OD flow via
Propositions~\ref{prop:phi} and~\ref{prop:transfer}.
Three caveats bound the claim's scope.
The bound is one-directional: high retention is (approximately) necessary for
lossless regional decomposition, not sufficient, and METIS also retains
substantial flow --- the theory predicts orderings and bounds, not that
InfoMap is optimal for $J$.
The constants are network- and regime-dependent.
And optimisation effects can dominate in practice: the chain bounds only the
coupling term $C(1 - \Psi)$; it does \emph{not} bound
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{cond}}(\mathcal{P})$, which the partition also moves.
The two channels are experimentally distinguishable --- severed coupling
requires causal inter-agent influence, whereas conditioning requires only
\emph{correlated} within-module signals --- and keeping them as separate,
named terms is what lets Section~\ref{sec:exp-perturb} report a regime in
which the coupling term is empirically near zero while the conditioning term,
by elimination, carries the observed training differences.
The theory predicts performance \emph{trends} across partitions, not
guaranteed outcomes of any single training run.

\paragraph{Per-level reading.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:valueloss} specialises differently at each coordination
coupling level of Section~\ref{sec:levels}, yielding level-specific
predictions.
\emph{L1 (regional reward).} A shared regional reward is a surrogate for each
agent's own reward whose bias grows with within-module outcome
\emph{misalignment}; the forward channel ties alignment to flow coherence, so
the flow partition should help most where reward coupling is rawest.
\emph{L2 (regional critic).} The regional critic is precisely the local model
of influence-based abstraction: its approximation loss is bounded by the
influence it ignores, i.e.\ the cut dependency~\citep{congeduti2021iba}.
\emph{L3 (attention masking).} The truncation bound carries a second term
that grows once modules shrink below the influence-decay length: masking then
removes edges the decay argument still needs.
This predicts a \emph{phase boundary} in the module count $K$: below it,
flow-coherent partitions win; above it, the truncation term dominates and
geometrically compact partitions can win, with the reversal point shifting
with network size and demand level.
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} tests this as a prediction rather than treating
any observed reversal as an anomaly.

          Within-module reward averaging operates on correlated signals, reducing
          gradient noise and training instability.
    \item[\textbf{OP2 (L2):}]
          $\Psi(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{InfoMap}}, D^{Q}) >
              \Psi(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{METIS}}, D^{Q})$.
          The regional critic captures dominant value dependencies without modelling
          irrelevant cross-module states, reducing credit-assignment variance.
    \item[\textbf{OP3 (L3):}]
          $\Psi(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{InfoMap}}, D^{G}) >
              \Psi(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{METIS}}, D^{G})$, observed via a lower
          influence-weighted attention range $\hat{\rho}_u$ for regional vs.\ global
          CoLight (Section~\ref{sec:attnrange}), provided module size is
          sufficient (${\geq}4$ agents per module).
\end{description}

\noindent The three OPs together constitute \textbf{Hypothesis H}: an InfoMap
partition achieves higher $\Psi$ than METIS across the three dependency
instruments, and --- per the monotone bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:valueloss} ---
this advantage predicts better task-performance trends at each coupling level,
\emph{subject to} the L3 module-size phase boundary identified in
Section~\ref{sec:valueloss}.
The qualifier is load-bearing, not decorative: Section~\ref{sec:experiments}
reports a $\Psi(D^{G})$ reversal at the coarser tested module count on both
networks, which OP3's own phase-boundary clause predicts rather than
contradicts, while OP1 and OP2 hold in every same-$k$ comparison tested.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
We evaluate on two real-world SUMO road networks of different scale:
Cologne8 (8 signalised intersections, 30-dimensional per-agent state) and
Ingolstadt21 (21 signalised intersections, 85-dimensional per-agent state).
For each network we construct an InfoMap partition and a METIS partition at
matched module count~$k$, plus a no-partition (global/local) baseline, and
hold the partition fixed while varying the coordination mechanism across the
three coupling levels of Section~\ref{sec:levels}: IPPO (L1), MAPPO (L2), and
CoLight (L3), with IDQN deferred per Section~\ref{sec:levels}.
$D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ is estimated by the KSG estimator~\citep{kraskov2004ksg}
over lags $\tau \in \{1,5,15,30\}$ steps ($k_{\mathrm{KSG}}=3$) from a
361-step warm-up trajectory, repeated over 3 stochastic seeds per network;
$D^{G}$ is the Jacobian instrument of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dg} evaluated on one
seed-7 global (no-partition) CoLight checkpoint per network, and therefore
carries no across-seed uncertainty estimate.
Downstream training results are single-seed (seed~0, 1,500 episodes each),
reported here as a case study rather than a statistically powered comparison;
we state this scope explicitly rather than invoking rliable-style IQM and
bootstrap confidence intervals~\citep{agarwal2021rliable} over data we do not
have, in line with the standard of evidence Henderson et al.\
argue RL results should be held to~\citep{henderson2018matters}.
A second data collection supports the community-detection benchmark of
Section~\ref{sec:exp-benchmark}: 10 stochastic-policy episodes at each of 5
traffic seeds per network, split chronologically 6/2/2 within each seed into
construction, validation, and held-out test episodes.
A third supports the randomized action-perturbation probe of
Section~\ref{sec:exp-perturb}: 400 mixed-arm and 100 single-agent
intervention episodes under the frozen global CoLight checkpoints, with the
same chronological split discipline.
We therefore split the evaluation into two parts: retention diagnostics
(Sections~\ref{sec:exp-phi}--\ref{sec:exp-perturb}), which are multi-seed
and constitute the paper's confirmatory evidence where the underlying test is
resolved (the spill-back and action-influence diagnostics are reported as
unresolved), and downstream training performance
(Sections~\ref{sec:exp-perf}--\ref{sec:attnrange}), which is single-seed and
descriptive.

\subsection{Constrained Community-Detection Benchmark with Held-Out Selection}
\label{sec:exp-benchmark}

The comparisons so far fix InfoMap as the flow-based method and METIS as the
topological baseline.
Two questions remain open: whether whole-network InfoMap is the right
\emph{optimiser} once the operational constraints motivated in
Section~\ref{sec:ndpomdp} --- bounded module size for coordination
tractability, and spatially connected modules for operability --- are imposed
as hard feasibility conditions, and whether retention advantages survive a
selection protocol in which held-out data are touched only after selection.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Using the 6/2/2 construction/validation/test traces of
Section~\ref{sec:setup}, we build five dependency graphs per network and
traffic seed --- realised flow $\mathbf{W}$, full-state $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$,
queue-conditioned $D^{\mathrm{CMI}}$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:dcmi}),
global-checkpoint Jacobian $D^{G}$ averaged over 100 split-specific
observations, and their equal-mass combination --- and partition each with
six algorithms: the legacy intersection-level InfoMap partitions of
Table~\ref{tab:phi}, directed agent-level InfoMap, normalised spectral
cut~\citep{shimalik2000}, Leiden under the constant Potts
model~\citep{traag2019leiden,traag2011cpm}, directed Markov
stability~\citep{delvenne2010stability,lambiotte2014multiscale}, and
METIS~\citep{karypis1998metis}, at 50 optimisation seeds each and 41
resolution or Markov-time scales where the algorithm has one.
Feasibility requires exactly $k$ modules ($k \in \{2,\ldots,4\}$ on Cologne8,
$\{3,\ldots,7\}$ on Ingolstadt21), module sizes in
$[2, \lceil 1.5N/k \rceil]$, and physical connectivity; infeasible outputs
are boundary-repaired where possible or recorded as explicit failures.
Of 337{,}575 candidate records, 62{,}257 were accepted, yielding 4{,}935
unique feasible partitions, each scored against 1{,}000 exact-size
unrestricted and 1{,}000 exact-size connected nulls (Section~\ref{sec:psi}).
Pareto selection --- maximising connected-null-adjusted retention while
minimising normalised cut, imbalance, fragmentation, and communication cost
--- uses validation data only.

\paragraph{Finding 1: the legacy partitions are not universally feasible.}
The Cologne8 flow-$k3$ partition ($6/1/1$) violates both size bounds, and the
Ingolstadt21 flow-$k5$ partition contains a singleton and a spatially
disconnected module; only Ingolstadt21 flow-$k4$ is feasible, and on held-out
queue-CMI it retains $0.2566$ raw but only $0.0047$ after connected-null
adjustment, and does not reach the validation Pareto front.
Whole-network InfoMap, run unconstrained, thus optimises codelength at the
expense of constraints an operational deployment cannot waive.

\paragraph{Finding 2: action-level CMI is not statistically resolved.}
In all 60 map/seed/split matrix tests, no action$\to$future-queue or
action$\to$future-reward edge survives Benjamini--Hochberg
correction~\citep{benjamini1995fdr}; the smallest adjusted $q$-values are
$0.1118$ (Cologne8) and $0.2794$ (Ingolstadt21).
Bias-corrected, unthresholded CMI can still rank partitions in an exploratory
sense, but nothing in these traces certifies causal control coupling.
This bounds the empirical reach of the
Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}--Theorem~\ref{thm:valueloss} chain:
dependency retention is confirmed at the state level ($D^{\mathrm{MI}}$) and
at the learned-model level ($D^{G}$), and remains unresolved at the action
level.
Section~\ref{sec:exp-perturb} sharpens this finding with a randomized design
that removes the observational identification problem --- and reaches the
same null.

\paragraph{Finding 3: no single optimiser dominates.}
The Cologne8 validation front contains 23 records but only five unique
partitions, all at $k{=}2$; the same high-retention partition is recovered by
agent-level InfoMap, normalised cut, Leiden, Markov stability, and METIS run
on different dependency graphs.
The Ingolstadt21 front contains 27 records and 17 unique partitions

METIS; Markov stability and the legacy partitions are absent from it.
The choice of dependency graph, not the choice of optimiser, is the
load-bearing decision --- consistent with the paper's framing that what a
partition should \emph{retain} is the primary question.

\paragraph{Finding 4: retention trades off against balance, and validation
    ordering does not fully generalise.}
Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-frontier} summarises the front.

\paragraph{Finding 5: stability and sensitivity.}
Median optimisation stability across 50 seeds (ARI) is $1.000$ on Cologne8
and $0.806$ on Ingolstadt21; median stability across the five traffic seeds
is $0.909$ and $0.698$.

optimisation stability alone is insufficient, since the perfectly stable
Markov-stability partitions do not reach the Ingolstadt21 front.

and mean pairwise VI $\leq 0.1$) exists on Ingolstadt21 only for the flow,
Jacobian, and combined graphs --- not for $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ or
$D^{\mathrm{CMI}}$.
Relaxing the maximum-size bound enlarges the feasible set by only $0.5\%$ on
Cologne8 but $8.3\%$ on Ingolstadt21, so the hard balance bound binds mainly
on the larger network; we retain the hard-bound result as primary and the
minimum-size-only variant as sensitivity analysis.

\paragraph{What survives.}
The matched-$k$ retention ordering flow~$>$~METIS of
Sections~\ref{sec:exp-phi}--\ref{sec:exp-psi-mi} is untouched by this
benchmark: it is a statement about two fixed partitions on identical data.
What the benchmark forecloses is the stronger reading that whole-network
InfoMap is the partitioning method of choice: under operational feasibility
constraints its partitions can be infeasible outright, no optimiser dominates
the constrained front, and held-out significance is at best partial.
The durable contribution is the evaluation frame itself --- retention metrics
with exact-size and connected nulls, validation-only Pareto selection, and
held-out reporting --- which any future partitioning proposal can be scored
under.
The identification half of the natural next step has since been executed:
Section~\ref{sec:exp-perturb} reports a preregistered randomized
action-perturbation experiment that removes the confounding problem --- and
still finds no FDR-significant edge on held-out data.
Preregistered policy tests of a small validation-front subset spanning the
retention--balance trade-off remain future work.

\subsection{Randomized Action-Perturbation Probe of Control Dependency}
\label{sec:exp-perturb}

The observational null of Finding 2 admits two readings: an identification
failure (policy actions are near-deterministic functions of states, so
conditioning cannot separate action influence from state influence), or
genuinely weak control coupling.
To separate them we ran a preregistered randomized intervention on the frozen
global CoLight checkpoint of each network, with no retraining.

\paragraph{Design.}
In the mixed arm, each agent's action is independently replaced with a
uniformly random valid phase with probability $0.20$ at every decision step;
the \emph{logged random assignment} $\delta_i^t \in \{0, 1\}$ --- exogenous
by construction --- is the exposure, not the policy action.
(The realised action-change rate is $0.131$--$0.135$, since the random draw
can coincide with the policy's choice; power is set by the changed rate, not
the assigned one.)
A single-agent arm, perturbing one agent at a time, checks sensitivity to
simultaneous interventions.
We collected 400 mixed-arm and 100 single-agent episodes, split
chronologically $120/40/40$ and $30/10/10$ per network into
construction/validation/held-out sets.
The primary family was fixed before collection: physical-adjacency and
observed-flow pairs (22 on Cologne8, 71 on Ingolstadt21) at
travel-time-derived lags, with Benjamini--Hochberg correction confined to
this family and hop-${\geq}3$ pairs reserved as negative controls.
Two estimators are reported per directed pair: a regression-adjusted average
treatment effect of $\delta_i^t$ on $q_j^{t+\tau}$ (adjusting for $q_j^t$;
$D^{\mathrm{ATE}}$ is its standardised absolute value) and the randomized CMI
$\hat{I}(\delta_i^t;\, q_j^{t+\tau} \mid q_j^t)$ with Jeffreys smoothing, 500
within-episode circular shifts, and 1{,}000 episode bootstraps.

\paragraph{Result: identification fixed, null persists.}
The design checks pass: the intervention measurably randomised behaviour
(mean held-out action entropy rose from $0.52$--$0.55$ to $0.79$ nats), and
the hop-${\geq}3$ negative controls show raw false-positive rates of
$0.00$--$0.05$, at the nominal level.
Yet on held-out data there are \emph{zero} FDR-significant edges on either
network under either estimator.
The single construction-split discovery (one Cologne8 CMI edge, $0.00025$
nats, $q = 0.044$) does not replicate on held-out data; the single-agent arm
finds nothing; and mixed-versus-single ATE sign agreement is $0.36$--$0.47$
--- chance level, the signature of noise-dominated estimates of near-zero
effects at sub-vehicle scale (arm-to-arm ATE differences average
$0.09$--$0.15$ queued vehicles).
One caveat is disclosed rather than argued away: the prospective power
calculation called for $165$ (Cologne8) and $194$ (Ingolstadt21) construction
episodes against the $120$ supplied, so only $14/22$ and $41/71$ pairs
reached the planned $80\%$ power --- null edges outside that subset are
inconclusive, not evidence of exact independence.
The powered subsets, however, also produced zero held-out discoveries.

\paragraph{Exploratory partition-level concentration.}
Cross-evaluating the \emph{continuous} (unthresholded) $D^{\mathrm{ATE}}$ and
$D^{\mathrm{CMI}}$ weights on the existing validation-front partitions
against the stored exact-size connected nulls, one Ingolstadt21 front
partition ($k{=}3$) reaches held-out $\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}} = 0.592$ (nominal
$p = 0.020$) on $D^{\mathrm{ATE}}$ and $0.918$ ($p = 0.009$) on
$D^{\mathrm{CMI}}$.
As post-hoc maxima over the 23 cross-evaluated partitions these are not
selection-corrected (a global correction leaves them non-significant), both
significant-edge matrices have zero mass, and we report them as exploratory
only.
They do, however, repeat the aggregate-versus-pairwise lesson of H1 at the
causal level: pooled partition-level statistics may carry power that
edge-level FDR destroys, so the preregistered test for any future collection
should be a \emph{single} partition-level hypothesis --- the
$\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}$ of one named front partition against the connected null
--- rather than another edge family.

\paragraph{What the null means for the chain.}
Three readings, not mutually exclusive, survive.
\emph{Closed-loop damping:} the perturbation is applied while every other
intersection runs a trained stabilising policy, so what is measured is
closed-loop influence --- which competent control attenuates by design; the
coordination question during \emph{learning} concerns a different, larger
coupling.
\emph{Demand regime:} this is the third independent null probe --- after the
spill-back diagnostic and the observational CMI --- converging on a deeply
undersaturated regime in which both transmission channels are nearly inert,
consistent with the transfer bound's own constants locating strong coupling
near saturation.
\emph{Residual power}, per the caveat above.
The consequence for Theorem~\ref{thm:valueloss} is clarifying rather than
damaging: if the influence mass available to cut is itself near zero in this
regime, the coupling term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:valueloss} cannot distinguish
partitions, and the theorem's decomposition locates the large
partition-dependent training differences of Section~\ref{sec:exp-perf} in the
conditioning term $\epsilon_{\mathrm{cond}}(\mathcal{P})$ ---
\emph{optimisation conditioning} --- the partition-dependent channel the
chain names but does not bound.
Regional reward averaging needs \emph{correlated} within-module signals, not
causally coupled ones, and correlation (including its common-demand
component) is precisely what the confirmed $\Psi(D^{\mathrm{MI}})$ retention
supplies; the regional critic likewise needs a smaller input, not a causally
closed one.
The claim this paper can defend is therefore that flow-coherent partitions
\emph{condition learning}; whether they additionally preserve causal coupling
that matters near saturation is an open question this experiment has
sharpened but not answered.

CoLight is treated throughout this paper as a case study of the L3 coupling

\subsection{Scope of Ablations}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper asked what a partition of a traffic network should preserve, and
answered with a retention chain whose epistemic status we can state link by
link.
The map equation's inter-module rate \emph{is} the cut flow --- an exact
identity (Proposition~\ref{prop:phi}), making InfoMap a maximiser of
entropy-regularised flow retention.
Under flow-local dynamics, cut flow upper-bounds cut dependency --- a theorem
with explicit constants in a stylised link-queue model whose assumptions are
the paper's assumptions made literal
(Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}, Appendix~\ref{app:proofs:transfer}).
Cut dependency upper-bounds regional value loss --- imported machinery,
defended as such and never as a proved result
(Theorem~\ref{thm:valueloss}).
The chain's testable prediction is confirmed at the retention level: flow
partitions retain more flow and more state mutual information than matched
METIS partitions in every one of 12 same-$k$ comparisons across two networks
and three seeds, with H1 and H2 behaving as the operational signatures ---
not gates --- the transfer bound says they are.

The paper's most consequential empirical finding is a disciplined negative.
A preregistered randomized action intervention --- which removes the
observational identification problem outright and passed its calibration
checks --- finds no resolvable action-level coupling in the tested demand
regime, converging with the empty spill-back diagnostic and the
observational CMI null.
The decomposition of Theorem~\ref{thm:valueloss} then locates the large
single-seed training gains we observe under regional reward sharing and
regional critics in the conditioning term
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{cond}}(\mathcal{P})$: correlated within-module signals ---
precisely what the confirmed $\Psi(D^{\mathrm{MI}})$ retention supplies ---
reduce credit-assignment variance and critic approximation burden, no causal
coupling required.
The claim this paper defends is therefore that flow-coherent partitions
\emph{condition learning}; whether they additionally preserve causal coupling
that matters near saturation is tested-and-null in this regime and open in
general.
The CoLight case study bounds the claim from the other side: below roughly
four agents per module, attention masking --- not flow coherence --- becomes
the binding constraint, exactly the phase boundary the per-level reading of
Theorem~\ref{thm:valueloss} predicts.

The held-out benchmark bounds the claim's scope as an algorithm
recommendation: under operational feasibility constraints, no detector
dominates the Pareto front, whole-network InfoMap partitions can be
infeasible outright, and the choice of \emph{dependency graph} is the
load-bearing decision.
What survives is the criterion and its evaluation frame --- retention metrics
with exact-size and connectivity-preserving nulls, validation-only Pareto
selection, held-out reporting --- under which any future partitioning
proposal, learned or constructed, can be scored.
For a practitioner the frame implies a concrete procedure: build the OD flow
graph from trip data; run candidate detectors under the deployment's module
count, size, and connectivity constraints; score feasible candidates by
$\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}$ against the connected null on validation traces;
select on the validation Pareto front; and policy-test the selected
candidate before deployment.

Limitations, stated plainly.
The bounds are one-directional: retention is necessary, not sufficient, so
high $\Psi$ certifies nothing by itself --- the theory speaks about the
partitions it rules \emph{out}.
The constants $\kappa$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{dem}}$ are network- and
regime-dependent, and the conditional-MI fix that removes the demand
confounder exactly in the stylised model
(Corollary~\ref{cor:demzero}) has not yet been exercised empirically.
The memoryless walk approximates path-dependent routing, and directed
Infomap's teleportation shifts the walk the identity is exact for.
The OD matrix comes from a random-policy warm-up, an approximation to demand
under trained control.
The partition is static, under regime stationarity
(Assumption~\ref{ass:stationary}); real demand is not.
The training results are a single-seed case study, and the causal link of
the chain is theory-supported only.

Three next experiments are decisive rather than incremental: a higher-demand
arm that activates both transmission channels near saturation, where the
transfer bound's own constants say the coupling question has content; a
\emph{single} preregistered partition-level hypothesis --- the
$\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}$ of one named validation-front partition against the
connected null --- in place of edge families; and preregistered policy tests
of a small front subset spanning the retention--balance trade-off, closing
the loop the benchmark opened between selection and training.
Beyond these lie dynamic, regime-indexed repartitioning and real-world OD
data.
What a partition should retain is, on our evidence, the right question; the
regimes in which retention becomes causal rather than conditioning is the
question this paper hands to the next one.

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:phi}}
\label{app:proofs:phi}

With the empirical OD walk, the stationary probability of traversing the
directed edge $(i, j)$ in one step is
$p_i \, p_{ij} = \frac{\sum_b W_{ib}}{\sum_{a,b} W_{ab}} \cdot
    \frac{W_{ij}}{\sum_{b} W_{ib}} = \frac{W_{ij}}{\sum_{a,b} W_{ab}}$.
The aggregate inter-module rate is the total stationary probability of steps
whose endpoints lie in different modules,
$q_{\circlearrowleft} = \sum_{i,j:\, c(i) \neq c(j)} p_i \, p_{ij}
    = \big(\sum_{i,j:\, c(i) \neq c(j)} W_{ij}\big) / \sum_{a,b} W_{ab}$,
which is $1 - \Phi(\mathcal{P}, \mathbf{W})$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi}.
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}: complete proof in a stylised
    linear model}
\label{app:proofs:transfer}

The general statement of Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer} under
Assumptions~\ref{ass:local}--\ref{ass:stationary} is a conjecture; the tools
required for it are identified in the closing remark.
Here we prove the bound \emph{exactly}, with explicit constants, in a
stylised model in which the three assumptions hold literally.

\paragraph{The stylised model.}
Let $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^N$ collect per-intersection queue deviations from an
undersaturated operating point, evolving as the linearisation of a
link-queue/CTM model~\citep{daganzo1994ctm,jin2012linkqueue} about that
point:
\begin{equation}
    x(t+1) \;=\; A\, x(t) \;+\; b\, \xi(t) \;+\; w(t),
    \label{eq:var}
\end{equation}
under four conditions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item[(M1)] \emph{Locality} (Assumption~\ref{ass:local}):
          $A_{ij} \neq 0$ only if $j \in \mathcal{N}(i) \cup \{i\}$.
    \item[(M2)] \emph{Flow-bounded coupling} (Assumption~\ref{ass:coupling}):
          $|A_{ij}| \leq \alpha \widetilde{W}_{ij}$ for $i \neq j$, with the
          common-demand coupling $\beta_{ij}$ carried by the loading vector
          $b$ rather than by $A$.
    \item[(M3)] \emph{Sub-criticality}:
          $\max\!\left(\|\,|A|\,\|_1, \|\,|A|\,\|_\infty\right) \leq 1 -
              \delta$ for some $\delta \in (0, 1]$ --- the total coupling any
          intersection exerts on, and receives from, its neighbours is
          bounded below one; this is the undersaturated-regime condition.
    \item[(M4)] \emph{Noise and demand} (Assumption~\ref{ass:stationary} via
          time-invariance): $w(t) \stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}
              \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_w)$ with diagonal $\Sigma_w$,
          $\sigma_{\min}^2 \leq (\Sigma_w)_{ii} \leq \sigma_{\max}^2$; the
          scalar demand factor $\xi(t) \stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}
              \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_\xi^2)$ is independent of $w$.
\end{itemize}
The stationary lagged covariance factorises as
$\Gamma_\tau := \operatorname{Cov}\!\left(x(t{+}\tau), x(t)\right)
    = A^\tau \Sigma$ with
$\Sigma = \Sigma_0 + \Sigma_\xi$, where
$\Sigma_0 = \sum_{k \geq 0} A^k \Sigma_w (A^{\!\top})^k$ is the noise-driven
part and
$\Sigma_\xi = \sigma_\xi^2 \sum_{k \geq 0} A^k b\, b^{\!\top} (A^{\!\top})^k$
the demand-driven part; $\bar{\sigma}^2 := \max_i \Sigma_{ii} < \infty$ by
(M3).
The instrument is the per-lag Gaussian MI
$D^{\mathrm{MI}}_{ij} = I\!\left(x_i(t);\, x_j(t{+}\tau)\right)$ for
$\tau \geq 1$; the lag-averaged instrument used in the experiments obeys the
same bound because the bound below is uniform over $\tau \geq 1$.

\begin{lemma}[Cut charging]
    \label{lem:cutcharge}
    For every partition $\mathcal{P}$ and every $\tau \geq 1$,
    \begin{equation*}
        \sum_{c(i) \neq c(j)} \left|\left(A^\tau \Sigma_0\right)_{ji}\right|
        \;\leq\;
        \frac{2\, \sigma_{\max}^2\, \alpha}{\delta^3}
        \sum_{c(u) \neq c(v)} \widetilde{W}_{uv}.
    \end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Expand
    $(A^\tau \Sigma_0)_{ji}
        = \sum_{k \geq 0} \sum_m (A^{\tau+k})_{jm} (\Sigma_w)_{mm}
        (A^{k})_{im}$
    and bound entrywise by $|A|$: each term pairs a walk $m \to i$ of length
    $k$ with a walk $m \to j$ of length $\tau + k$ in the coupling graph.
    If $c(i) \neq c(j)$, at least one of the two walks crosses the partition:
    if $c(m) = c(i)$, the walk to $j$ crosses; otherwise the walk to $i$
    crosses (the degenerate $k = 0$ term forces $m = i$ and falls in the
    first case, which is why $\tau \geq 1$ suffices).
    Decompose each crossing walk at its \emph{first} crossing step
    $(u \to v)$, whose weight obeys $|A_{vu}| \leq \alpha \widetilde{W}_{uv}$
    by (M2); the prefix and suffix are unconstrained walks.
    Since all terms are non-negative, the coupled sum over lengths is bounded
    by the decoupled product of resolvent sums: summing the prefix over its
    free start node and all lengths gives a row sum of
    $(I - |A|)^{-1}$, at most $1/\delta$ by (M3); summing the suffix over its
    free end node gives a column sum, at most $1/\delta$; and summing the
    non-crossing walk over its free endpoint and all lengths gives another
    factor $1/\delta$.
    Collecting the two cases yields the factor $2$, and
    $(\Sigma_w)_{mm} \leq \sigma_{\max}^2$ completes the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Gaussian MI is linear in correlation under a noise floor]
    \label{lem:gaussmi}
    Let $\bar{\rho}^2 := 1 - \sigma_{\min}^2 / \bar{\sigma}^2$.
    Then for all pairs $(i, j)$ and all $\tau \geq 1$, the lagged correlation
    obeys $|\rho_{ij}(\tau)| \leq \bar{\rho} < 1$ and
    \begin{equation*}
        D^{\mathrm{MI}}_{ij}
        = -\tfrac{1}{2} \ln\!\left(1 - \rho_{ij}^2(\tau)\right)
        \;\leq\;
        \frac{\bar{\rho}}{2 (1 - \bar{\rho}^2)}\, |\rho_{ij}(\tau)|
        \;\leq\;
        \frac{\bar{\rho}}{2 (1 - \bar{\rho}^2)\, \sigma_{\min}^2}
        \left|\left(\Gamma_\tau\right)_{ji}\right|.
    \end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    For $\tau \geq 1$, $x_i(t)$ is measurable with respect to the history up
    to $t + \tau - 1$, so
    $\operatorname{Var}\!\left(x_j(t{+}\tau) \mid x_i(t)\right)
        \geq (\Sigma_w)_{jj} \geq \sigma_{\min}^2$,
    while $\operatorname{Var}\!\left(x_j(t{+}\tau)\right) \leq
        \bar{\sigma}^2$; hence
    $1 - \rho_{ij}^2 \geq \sigma_{\min}^2 / \bar{\sigma}^2$, i.e.\
    $|\rho_{ij}| \leq \bar{\rho}$.
    The secant bound $-\ln(1 - u) \leq u / (1 - \bar{u})$ for
    $0 \leq u \leq \bar{u} < 1$, applied with $u = \rho^2$ and
    $\bar{u} = \bar{\rho}^2$, together with $\rho^2 \leq \bar{\rho}\,|\rho|$,
    gives the first inequality; the second follows because both standard
    deviations are at least $\sigma_{\min}$
    (as $\Sigma \succeq \Sigma_w \succeq \sigma_{\min}^2 I$).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Transfer bound, stylised model]
    \label{prop:transfer-linear}
    Under (M1)--(M4), for every partition $\mathcal{P}$ and every lag
    $\tau \geq 1$ (hence for the lag-averaged instrument),
    \begin{equation*}
        1 - \Psi(\mathcal{P}, D^{\mathrm{MI}})
        \;\leq\;
        \kappa \left(1 - \Phi(\mathcal{P}, \widetilde{\mathbf{W}})\right)
        + \epsilon_{\mathrm{dem}},
    \end{equation*}
    with explicit constants
    \begin{equation*}
        \kappa
        = \frac{\bar{\rho}\, \sigma_{\max}^2\, \alpha}
        {(1 - \bar{\rho}^2)\, \sigma_{\min}^2\, \delta^3}
        \cdot
        \frac{\sum_{u \neq v} \widetilde{W}_{uv}}
        {\sum_{i \neq j} D^{\mathrm{MI}}_{ij}},
        \qquad
        \epsilon_{\mathrm{dem}}
        = \frac{\bar{\rho}}{2 (1 - \bar{\rho}^2)\, \sigma_{\min}^2}
        \cdot
        \frac{\sum_{c(i) \neq c(j)}
            \left|\left(A^\tau \Sigma_\xi\right)_{ji}\right|}
        {\sum_{i \neq j} D^{\mathrm{MI}}_{ij}}.
    \end{equation*}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    Split $\Gamma_\tau = A^\tau \Sigma_0 + A^\tau \Sigma_\xi$, apply
    Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussmi} to each cut pair, bound the noise-driven cut mass
    by Lemma~\ref{lem:cutcharge}, leave the demand-driven cut mass as
    $\epsilon_{\mathrm{dem}}$'s numerator, and divide by the total dependency
    mass $\sum_{i \neq j} D^{\mathrm{MI}}_{ij}$, using
    $1 - \Phi = \sum_{c(u) \neq c(v)} \widetilde{W}_{uv} \big/
        \sum_{u \neq v} \widetilde{W}_{uv}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Demand conditioning removes the confounder exactly]
    \label{cor:demzero}
    Conditioned on the demand path $\{\xi_s\}$, the process~\eqref{eq:var}
    has the same law with $\sigma_\xi = 0$; hence $\Sigma_\xi = 0$ and the
    conditional-MI instrument satisfies
    Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer-linear} with
    $\epsilon_{\mathrm{dem}} = 0$.
    Aggregate network occupancy is the operational proxy for $\xi$
    recommended in Section~\ref{sec:transfer}.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Scope, and what remains open.}
The model delivers Assumptions~\ref{ass:local}--\ref{ass:stationary}
literally: (M1) is flow locality, (M2) is flow-bounded coupling with the
demand channel isolated in $b$, and time-invariance of all parameters is
regime stationarity.
Three limits of the result should be stated plainly.
First, it is a linearisation about an undersaturated operating point: the
constant degrades as $\delta^{-3}$, so the bound is loosest approaching
saturation --- exactly where the backward (spill-back) channel activates.
This is a property the general theory shares (Section~\ref{sec:transfer}),
now visible in a constant rather than hidden in a sketch.
Second, the statement is for scalar per-agent states, matching the queue
instruments; vector-valued states extend via the log-determinant form of
Gaussian MI at the cost of heavier notation.
Third, the general nonlinear, non-Gaussian case remains a conjecture: strong
data-processing inequalities for Bayesian networks --- information
percolation bounds of the Evans--Schulman
type~\citep{evans1999percolation,polyanskiy2017sdpi} --- provide precisely
the path-sum-of-contraction-products structure that
Lemma~\ref{lem:cutcharge} instantiates linearly, and are the natural route to
removing the linearity assumption.
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{dem}}$ is not small in general and grows with network
size; when it dominates, the bound is uninformative --- which is detectable,
and correctable by conditioning (Corollary~\ref{cor:demzero}).

\subsection{Theorem~\ref{thm:valueloss} (derivation notes)}
\label{app:proofs:valueloss}

Two independent routes give the stated shape; we import both rather than
re-derive them.
\emph{Exponential-decay route.}
Assumption~\ref{ass:coupling} bounds one-step influence entrywise; under the
$(c, \rho)$-exponential-decay conditions of \citet{qu2022scalable} (or the
policy-dependent spectral certificate $\rho(H^{\pi}) < 1$ of
\citet{chakraborty2026locality}, which weakens the row-sum condition), the
sensitivity of agent $i$'s value to agent $j$ decays exponentially in graph
distance, and truncating each agent's policy/value dependence to its module is
an approximation whose bias is bounded by the discarded influence mass ---
which is, by construction, the cut dependency that $1 - \Psi$ measures.
\emph{Influence-based-abstraction route.}
A regional critic is a local model that approximates (or ignores) the
influence sources outside its module; \citet{congeduti2021iba} bound the value
loss of such a model by its influence-approximation error, which the cut
dependency mass upper-bounds.
The $\epsilon_{\mathrm{approx}}$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}^{0}$ terms
isolate neural-approximation and training-noise effects that the partition
does not control; the conditioning term
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{cond}}(\mathcal{P})$ isolates the partition's effect on
optimisation geometry, which the chain names but does not bound ---
Section~\ref{sec:exp-perturb} measures it by elimination.
Together they are why the theorem predicts trends across partitions rather
than outcomes of single runs.

\section{Algorithm: InfoMap Coordination Graph Construction}
\label{app:algorithm}

H1 and H2 are \emph{operational signatures} of the transfer bound
(Section~\ref{sec:transfer}), not engineering controls.
The algorithm always returns the InfoMap partition; the signature results are
reported as supporting evidence and logged for transparency.
A failure on a given network localises which transmission channel is inactive
there (e.g.\ an all-free-flow regime yields no spill-back events, so the
backward channel is inert) and is reported separately.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
    \caption{Flow-coherent coordination graph construction with dependency verification}
    \label{alg:infomap}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require Traffic network $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$,
        number of modules $K$, warm-up steps $T_w$
        \Ensure  Partition $\mathcal{M} = \{M_1, \ldots, M_K\}$,\;
        dependency matrix $\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{MI}}$,\;
        verification results $(p_{\mathrm{H1}},\, p_{\mathrm{H2}})$
        \State Run $T_w$ steps under a uniformly random policy; log all vehicle trips
        \State Construct $\mathbf{W}$: $\;W_{ij} \leftarrow$ vehicle count $i \to j$
        \State Apply Infomap to $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{OD}} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}, \mathbf{W})$
        $\;\Rightarrow\;$ partition $\mathcal{M} = \{M_1,\ldots,M_K\}$
        \State Estimate $D^{\mathrm{MI}}_{ij}$ via KSG estimator~\citep{kraskov2004ksg}
        at $\tau \in \{1,5,15,30\}$ steps
        \Statex \hspace{1em}\textit{// H2 signature check (backward channel)}
        \State Extract spill-back events from SUMO queue trajectories
        \State Compute within-module and cross-module spill-back proportions;
        run one-sample proportion test $\;\Rightarrow\; p_{\mathrm{H2}}$
        \Statex \hspace{1em}\textit{// H1 signature check (forward channel)}
        \State Classify agent pairs as within-module or cross-module under $\mathcal{M}$
        \State Compare $D^{\mathrm{MI}}_{ij}$ distributions;
        run Wilcoxon signed-rank test $\;\Rightarrow\; p_{\mathrm{H1}}$
        \State \textbf{Report} $(p_{\mathrm{H1}},\, p_{\mathrm{H2}})$;
        generate MI heatmap with module boundaries overlaid
        \State \Return $\mathcal{M}$,\; $\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{MI}}$,\;
        $(p_{\mathrm{H1}},\, p_{\mathrm{H2}})$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Regional MARL Architecture}
\label{app:arch}

All three coordination levels share a common regional infrastructure.
Partition assignments are stored in \texttt{cfg.management} (one list of
agent IDs per module), which is populated from the InfoMap or METIS output
before any training begins.
A central helper module (\texttt{agents/common/marl.py}) provides
\texttt{transform\_reward\_array(scope, rewards, cfg)} --- supporting
\texttt{local}, \texttt{global\_mean}, and \texttt{regional\_mean} scopes ---
and \texttt{CentralizedInputBuilder}, which assembles critic observations for
\texttt{global}, \texttt{regional}, or \texttt{local} scopes and zero-pads
uneven regional input sizes to a common dimension.

\subsection{L1 --- Regional Reward (IPPO)}
\label{app:arch:ippo}

Regional IPPO applies flow-partition structure at the reward level only.
Each agent maintains an independent local actor-critic PPO learner
(\texttt{agents/policy/pfrl\_ppo.py}); there is no cross-agent parameter
sharing or communication.
Before each learner's \texttt{observe()} call, individual scalar rewards
are passed through \texttt{transform\_reward\_array} with
\texttt{reward\_scope: regional\_mean}, replacing each agent's reward with
the arithmetic mean of rewards within its InfoMap module.
Unassigned agents (singletons not covered by \texttt{cfg.management}) retain
their individual reward.
The default configuration (\texttt{config/agent.yaml}) sets
\texttt{reward\_scope: local}; regional behaviour is enabled by
\texttt{reward\_scope: regional\_mean}.
IDQN (\texttt{agents/action\_value/pfrl\_dqn.py}) follows the identical
reward-rewriting path.

\paragraph{Mechanism.}
Regional averaging reduces credit-assignment noise when agents within the
same module experience correlated outcomes (high $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$).
It is harmful when modules group uncorrelated agents, because the averaged
signal is less informative than the individual reward.
This makes IPPO performance at L1 a direct diagnostic of partition quality.

\subsection{L2 --- Regional Critic (MAPPO)}
\label{app:arch:mappo}

MAPPO is implemented in \texttt{agents/policy/actor\_critic.py} on top of
\texttt{ActorCriticBase}.
Actors use local observations and mask invalid actions for heterogeneous
action spaces (shared actor-network weights across agents).
The critic input is assembled by \texttt{CentralizedInputBuilder} under
\texttt{critic\_scope: regional}: each agent's critic receives the concatenated
observations of all agents in its InfoMap module, zero-padded to the maximum
regional size.
Reward sharing is controlled separately via \texttt{reward\_scope}; the
standard regional MAPPO configuration uses both
\texttt{critic\_scope: regional} and \texttt{reward\_scope: regional\_mean}.

Global MAPPO (\texttt{critic\_scope: global}) feeds all $N$ agents'
observations to a single shared critic.
For $N = 21$ (Ingolstadt21), the global critic input dimension is $21 \times
    d_{\mathrm{obs}}$, making it substantially harder to train than a regional
critic of dimension $|M_k| \times d_{\mathrm{obs}}$ where $|M_k| \approx 4$--$5$.

\paragraph{Mechanism.}
The regional critic captures dominant value dependencies
($D^Q_{i\leftarrow j}$ high within module) without modelling irrelevant
cross-module states, reducing critic approximation error and policy-gradient
variance.
On networks where a global critic is impractical (large $N$), regional InfoMap
critics can \emph{outperform} global CTDE, as observed on Ingolstadt21.

\subsection{L3 --- Regional Attention (CoLight)}
\label{app:arch:colight}

CoLight is implemented in \texttt{agents/action\_value/colight.py}.
The topology is built from \texttt{signal.yaml} downstream links as
bidirectional graph edges.
Two distinct regional variants exist; they test different scientific questions.

\paragraph{Variant A: PartitionedAttentionStack.}
Enabled by \texttt{colight\_partitioned\_message\_passing: True}.
One GAT attention stack per module is created; all stacks share the same
underlying network weights and a single replay buffer.
Attention is structurally restricted to within-module edges --- agents in
different modules cannot attend to each other.
This is the correct variant for testing whether InfoMap topology improves
the quality of \emph{communication}, as the learned $D^G$ Jacobians are
computed on a globally-unified model but message-passing is constrained.

\paragraph{Variant B: CoLightRegional.}
Separate agent class that instantiates one independent
\texttt{\_CoLightRegionLearner} per partition.
Each regional learner has its own CoLight network, target network, optimizer,
replay buffer, action mask, regional edge index, and $\epsilon$-schedule.
\texttt{CoLightRegional.observe()} converts individual rewards to the mean
reward within each region before inserting transitions into that region's
replay buffer.
This variant tests whether divide-and-conquer training by region is beneficial;
it is \emph{not} equivalent to restricting communication in a global model.
When module size is small ($|M_k| \lesssim 3$), Variant B creates highly
under-sampled regional learners with unstable target networks.

\paragraph{Fallback.}
If \texttt{cfg.management} is absent, both variants fall back to one global
region covering all agents.
