\begin{abstract}
    Coordinating traffic-signal agents in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) requires partitioning intersections into coordination modules before training, yet existing practice either inherits partitions from road-topology tools or learns them end-to-end for control return, without measuring what a partition actually preserves. We introduce a pre-training framework that scores candidate partitions by the vehicle flow and inter-agent dependency retained within their modules. We instantiate this framework using an origin-destination-induced signal-to-signal flow graph, making vehicle movement, rather than road geometry alone, the basis for constructing coordination regions. Across two SUMO networks, flow-based partitions retain more route flow and state dependency than matched topology-based partitions at every evaluated module count. In downstream experiments, the higher-retention partition achieves a lower best-checkpoint delay in six of eight matched MARL comparisons. These results support retention as a useful criterion for selecting coordination regions, although candidate partitions must also satisfy module-size and connectivity constraints, and our intervention tests do not establish causal coupling. We conclude that retention-guided partitioning provides an auditable, training-free design step for regional MARL and can reduce the number of candidate partitions requiring costly controller training.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Urban traffic signal control (TSC) is a canonical large-scale coordination problem: each signal acts on local observations, but a phase decision at one intersection reshapes arrival patterns and queue spill-back at its neighbors, so signals must be controlled jointly, naturally posing TSC as a multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) problem. Yet exact joint control requires evaluating a joint action space that grows exponentially with the number of intersections, which is intractable beyond a handful of agents~\citep{nair2005ndpomdp,kok2006ndpomdp,oliehoek2012ndpomdp}. This is why regional decomposition is the paradigm that makes MARL practical at scale~\citep{qu2020average,qu2022scalable}: the systems partition the network into coordination modules, whether explicit regions or implicit neighborhoods, and coordinate \emph{within} them, by shared rewards, centralized critics, communication, or hierarchy~\citep{ma2020fma2c,wei2019colight,xu2021hilight,zhu2026halo}. Every such system thus contains a \emph{coordination graph} fixed before training: a decision about which agents share coordination machinery. Because those mechanisms operate only within a module, any dependency the graph does not group together is invisible to the learner, so the partition itself determines what a system can coordinate. Unlike domains where physics dictates the interaction structure, in TSC this graph is a free design variable, and its design has received far less attention than the learning algorithms that run on top of it.

This design choice is rarely made deliberately: the coordination graph is typically inherited from general-purpose partitioners applied to road \emph{topology}, learned end-to-end for control return without stating what a good partition should retain, or borrowed from transport-planning zoning that is not connected to what learning agents require of a partition~\citep{laplacian2009ieee,communitydetection2021plosone,liu2023gplight,ma2022feudal,odflow2019partition,mfdpartition2020}.
Attention-based MARL~\citep{jiang2018atoc,wei2019colight} learns interaction structure implicitly instead, but gives no account of which dependencies are preserved or severed when attention is restricted to modules.
The gap spanning all three lines of work is measurement: \emph{partition choice is consequential and routinely made, but what a given partition keeps or discards is almost never quantified before training}, so competing choices cannot be compared except by running a full training loop on each, the most expensive step in the pipeline they are meant to precede.

This paper addresses that gap with three linked moves, detailed below: a training-free criterion for what a partition retains, a flow-based construction method that instantiates it, and an empirical test of the link between them.

\noindent\textbf{Main hypothesis.}
\emph{Among partitions of the same network at the same module count, higher flow retention is associated with higher inter-agent dependency retention, and higher retention tends to be associated with better regional MARL performance.}
Every measurement in this paper is organized around that claim. Section~\ref{sec:transfer} splits it into its two links, H1 from flow retention to dependency retention and H2 from dependency retention to learning outcomes, and Section~\ref{sec:experiments} tests each in turn.

We make three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A training-free partition score}
          (Section~\ref{sec:method}): a criterion that scores a candidate
          partition by how much observed route flow ($\Phi$) and
          inter-agent dependency ($\Psi$, measured three ways) it
          retains, so partitions can be compared before any controller
          is trained.
    \item \textbf{A flow-based construction method}
          (Section~\ref{sec:construction}): coordination modules built
          from a graph of vehicle trips between signals, not road
          adjacency, and partitioned with the map equation under size
          and connectivity limits. The resulting modules work with
          shared rewards, a shared critic, shared attention, or a
          feudal manager.
    \item \textbf{Evidence that retention predicts performance}
          (Sections~\ref{sec:exp-phi}--\ref{sec:exp-perf}): on both
          networks, flow-based partitions retain more route flow and
          state dependency than matched topology-based partitions at
          every module count tested, and higher flow retention tracks
          higher dependency retention. The higher-retention partition
          also trains to a lower delay in six of eight matched
          comparisons (Table~\ref{tab:wait-gains}), so retained flow is
          a useful signal for choosing a coordination partition, though
          not for every learner and network we tested.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

This section reviews prior work on traffic-network partitioning, MARL coordination topology, and inter-agent dependency measurement, and clarifies the gap addressed by our framework.

\paragraph{Network partitioning for traffic control}
Partitioning is already standard machinery in large-scale TSC, but the criterion is almost always structural or geometric: spectral partitioning of the road graph~\citep{laplacian2009ieee}, a manually assumed region decomposition in feudal MARL~\citep{ma2020fma2c}, adjacency-constrained star-topology regions~\citep{regionlight2024}, or congestion-feature and learned-similarity clustering~\citep{communitydetection2021plosone,liu2023gplight}. The closest departure, \citet{ma2022feudal}, learns a flow-based decomposition end-to-end for a feudal learner, but optimizes directly for control return, yielding no criterion that can be stated or audited independently of the learner. Flow-aware partitioning exists in the transport literature~\citep{odflow2019partition,mfdpartition2020}, but serves zoning and macroscopic modeling, not learning agents. Our gap claim is narrow: no prior work provides an \emph{explicit partition-quality criterion}, statable before training and independent of any learner, for what a coordination-graph designer should optimize.

\paragraph{Coordination topology in MARL}
A complementary literature learns or fixes interaction structure inside the learner instead of the coordination graph outside it: attentional and inferred communication~\citep{jiang2018atoc,i2c2021,peng2020structured,aamas2021correlated}, graph attention over road topology~\citep{wei2019colight}, and information-bottleneck or scalability-oriented topologies~\citep{hetib2025,sun2026ibvq,wang2020imac,expocomm2025,li2025topology,zhu2026halo}, which discover useful structure implicitly but give no external account of what is preserved or severed, since it lives inside the model and cannot be audited before training. Our approach fixes the partition from a training-free criterion, then uses the trained models' own dependency structure (Section~\ref{sec:psi}) to evaluate that partition after the fact.

\paragraph{Measuring inter-agent dependency}
We adapt three existing measurement families to a new question: model-free mutual information between agent states, whose mechanistic link to spill-back in TSC has been made explicit~\citep{dependencydyn2025}; Jacobian-based influence profiles from GNN and attention-attribution diagnostics~\citep{lrd2025,abnar2020rollout,simonyan2013saliency,sundararajan2017axiomatic}; and a retention ratio that aggregates any such matrix into a partition-level score against size-matched, connectivity-preserving nulls, which we did not find elsewhere in the MARL literature.

\paragraph{Where the expectation comes from} That retained flow and retained dependency should move together is not a new theorem here; it is assembled from cell-transmission and link-queue models of inter-signal coupling, data-processing/information-contraction arguments, and value-locality results in networked
MARL~\citep{daganzo1994ctm,jin2012linkqueue,polyanskiy2017sdpi,evans1999percolation,qu2020average,qu2022scalable,congeduti2021iba}; Section~\ref{sec:transfer} states the chain as H1 and H2 and names the confound that would break each. The map equation and Markov stability supply the flow-based partitioning objective itself~\citep{rosvall2008maps,rosvall2009mapequation,delvenne2010stability,lambiotte2014multiscale}.

\paragraph{Partitioning families and baselines} Partitioners for this task divide into two families by what they optimize. \emph{Topology-based} methods score a cut or a density contrast on the graph itself: normalized cut~\citep{shimalik2000}, Leiden~\citep{traag2019leiden,traag2011cpm}, and METIS~\citep{karypis1998metis}. \emph{Flow-based} methods instead score a dynamical process running on the weighted graph, so edge weights encode movement: the map equation~\citep{rosvall2008maps} and Markov stability~\citep{delvenne2010stability,lambiotte2014multiscale}. The map equation is the flow-based objective itself; InfoMap is the search algorithm that optimizes it~\citep{edler2023community}. Multiple algorithms instantiate each family, and our question is which \emph{criterion} to optimize, not which implementation wins, so the main comparisons use one widely-adopted representative per family: METIS for topology and directed InfoMap for flow. Results below are reported under each family's name, Topology and Flow.

\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

The methodology answers two questions and is split accordingly: this section asks what a coordination-graph partition should preserve and develops a training-free criterion and analysis model for it, while Section~\ref{sec:construction} asks how to build such a partition for traffic signal control, constructing candidates from OD demand and selecting among them under deployment constraints. The coordination graph is a design variable rather than a given (Section~\ref{sec:ndpomdp}), so a partition can be judged by what it preserves. Because coordination pressure in traffic is carried by vehicle flow, we score a partition by the OD flow it keeps inside modules, $\Phi_W$ (Section~\ref{sec:flowretention}), and by the dependency it keeps, $\Psi$, measured with three complementary instruments (Section~\ref{sec:psi}). Section~\ref{sec:transfer} states why these two scores, both computed before any learner runs, are expected to move together and to track downstream learning, and names the residual effects that would break the expectation.

\subsection{Coordination Graph as a Design Variable}
\label{sec:ndpomdp}

Each signalized intersection $i \in \mathcal{V}$ observes local queues, phase, and elapsed phase time $o_i$, and selects a phase action $a_i \in \mathcal{A}_i$. We model TSC as a network-distributed POMDP whose coordination graph $\mathcal{G}_C=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}_C)$ determines which agents share explicit coordination machinery.
The reward decomposes into local and interaction terms,
\begin{equation}
    R(\mathbf{a})
    =
    \sum_i r_i(o_i,a_i)
    +
    \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_C}
    r_{ij}(o_i,o_j,a_i,a_j)
    \label{eq:ndpomdp}
\end{equation}
Exact coordination over $\omega$ agents scales as $|\mathcal{A}|^\omega$, so practical MARL systems bound module size and decide which edges to keep before training. In traffic, the cost of cutting an edge is governed by vehicle movement: high-volume corridors create arrival coupling and, near saturation, spill-back coupling. The partitioning problem is therefore to keep the coordination graph small while retaining the vehicle flow that carries those dependencies.

Fig.~\ref{fig:threegraphs} separates three objects that are often blurred: the \emph{road graph} of physical adjacency, the \emph{OD-induced signal-transition flow graph} of vehicle exchange under a demand regime, and the \emph{coordination graph} of agents that share rewards, critic inputs, or messages.
Topology is not coordination pressure: two adjacent intersections with low traffic exchange may need little explicit coordination, while a high-volume corridor can carry dependency that an unweighted adjacency cut misses. The partition's role is to turn the route-flow graph into a coordination graph whose modules are small enough for MARL and coherent enough to keep important dependencies inside modules.

The framework below therefore keeps two terms separate: the coupling term that the retention theory bounds, and the optimization-conditioning term that Section~\ref{sec:transfer} introduces and the experiments treat as a leading working interpretation.

\subsection{Flow Retention and the Map Equation}
\label{sec:flowretention}

Let $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{flow}}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E},\mathbf{W})$ be the OD-induced signal-transition flow graph, where $W_{ij}$ is the number of vehicles traveling from signal $i$ to the next signal $j$ along realized routes during a representative warm-up window. The warm-up policy is uniformly random, so the graph reflects demand and network geometry before a learned controller reshapes the flow;
Section~\ref{sec:odconstruction} gives the construction.
For a partition $\mathcal{P}=\{P_1,\ldots,P_K\}$, define empirical OD-flow retention as
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_W(\mathcal{P})
    =
    \frac{\sum_k\sum_{i,j\in P_k} W_{ij}}
    {\sum_{i,j} W_{ij}}
    \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
We write $\Phi$ for $\Phi_W$ in the empirical tables. For another non-negative edge-weight matrix $\mathbf{X}$, $\Phi_W(\mathcal{P};\mathbf{X})$ denotes Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi} with $\mathbf{W}$ replaced by $\mathbf{X}$.

Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi} is a scoring rule. Any partitioner whose objective is defined on movement over $\mathbf{W}$, rather than on the geometry of the cut, is a candidate for scoring well under it; this is the flow-based family of Section~\ref{sec:related}. We work with the map equation~\citep{rosvall2008maps,rosvall2009mapequation} because its escape term equals the complement of retained \emph{stationary} flow (Eq.~\eqref{eq:qphi}), so the first link is an identity, not an approximation. Its coding objective still only favors the flow-retentive structure this hypothesis wants to preserve, without uniquely optimizing it.
For the random walk used by the map equation,
\begin{align}
    L(\mathcal{P})
     & =
    q_{\circlearrowleft}H(\mathcal{Q})
    +\sum_k p_k^{\circlearrowleft}H(\mathcal{P}_k)
    \label{eq:mapeq} \\
    q_{\circlearrowleft}
     & =
    1-\Phi_\pi(\mathcal{P})
    \label{eq:qphi}
\end{align}
where $\Phi_\pi(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{c(i)=c(j)}\pi_iP_{ij}$ is within-module stationary-walk mass, and $q_{\circlearrowleft}=1-\Phi_\pi(\mathcal{P})$ holds exactly for this stationary edge measure by construction of the map equation's escape term. The empirical $\Phi_W$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi} equals $\Phi_\pi$ when the OD edge measure matches the stationary edge measure. The entropy terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mapeq} prevent the trivial solution of one giant module, while the measured $\Phi_W$ reports how much observed traffic the final modules actually retain.

\subsection{Dependency Retention}
\label{sec:psi}

A partition that keeps flow should also keep the dependencies a learner can use.
For any non-negative dependency matrix $\mathbf{D}$, define
\begin{equation}
    \Psi(\mathcal{P},\mathbf{D})
    =
    \frac{\sum_k\sum_{i,j\in P_k} D_{ij}}
    {\sum_{i,j}D_{ij}}
    \label{eq:psi}
\end{equation}
the fraction of measured dependency retained within modules. Eq.~\eqref{eq:psi} is deliberately agnostic about what $\mathbf{D}$ contains: any non-negative pairwise dependency matrix can be scored, which is what lets the framework compare instruments that answer different questions on the same partition. Table~\ref{tab:notation} defines the three instruments used to score retention, $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$, $D^{G}$, and $D^{Q}$, plus a fourth, $D^{\mathrm{CMI}}$, used only as a diagnostic. Each instrument is matched to a coordination mechanism. $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ (KSG mutual information~\citep{kraskov2004ksg} between per-agent traffic-state slices, not the full network state vector) is the right instrument for regional reward sharing (level L1, Section~\ref{sec:levels}): independent actors have no cross-agent input path, so a Jacobian across agents would be structurally zero even if their traffic states are highly related. $D^{G}$ audits what a trained global attention model depends on, so it must be computed on a global, no-partition checkpoint; computing it on a partitioned model would bake in cross-module zeros and then rediscover the partition by construction. $D^{Q}$ applies the same Jacobian rule to a centralized MAPPO critic, measuring how strongly each agent's value estimate responds to every other agent's state features, and is the instrument aligned with regional critics (level L2, Section~\ref{sec:levels}). $D^{\mathrm{CMI}}$ conditions on the target agent's own queue and action to isolate one agent's causal effect on another's future queue. It targets causal control coupling, but we treat it only as a diagnostic: the effects it looks for turn out to be below what our data resolve (Section~\ref{sec:exp-perturb}).

\subsection{What We Expect Retention to Track}
\label{sec:transfer}

The main hypothesis of Section~\ref{sec:intro} is a chain: retained flow to retained dependency to regional MARL performance. This subsection separates it into the two links H1 and H2, states the grounds for each, and names the confound that would produce the same reading without the mechanism; Section~\ref{sec:experiments} tests them. They are hypotheses rather than theorems because a formal chain from flow retention to regional value loss needs linear flow-local dynamics, stationary demand, and an undersaturated regime, none of which is verifiable on these networks.

\noindent\textbf{H1: flow retention tracks dependency retention.}
Across candidate partitions of the same network at the same module count, a partition with higher $\Phi_W$ should also have higher $\Psi$, so that the dependency it discards falls as the flow it keeps rises. The reason is that inter-signal state coupling in traffic is carried by vehicles: in cell-transmission and link-queue models~\citep{daganzo1994ctm,jin2012linkqueue}, one intersection's phase decision reaches another only by moving traffic along the corridors between them, and information transmitted along a path attenuates with the capacity of the links it crosses~\citep{polyanskiy2017sdpi,evans1999percolation}. A cut placed on low-flow pairs should therefore sever less dependency than a cut of the same size placed without regard to flow. The obvious confound is common demand: a shared rush-hour profile correlates queues at intersections that exchange no vehicles, inflating $\Psi$ in a way that has nothing to do with what the partition retains. Section~\ref{sec:exp-relation} reports the relationship, and the Random-assignment control bounds how much of it common demand can explain.

\noindent\textbf{H2: dependency retention tracks learning outcomes.}
Among partitions of the same network at the same module count, the one that retains more dependency should yield better regional MARL training. Value-locality and influence-based-abstraction results for networked MARL~\citep{qu2020average,qu2022scalable,congeduti2021iba} give the structural version of this: a regional learner forfeits value roughly in proportion to the interaction strength it discards. There is also a second, non-exclusive route that does not require any surviving causal coupling at all. Regional reward sharing and regional critics consume the signals inside a module as a statistical object: grouping agents whose queues and delays move together reduces the variance of an averaged regional reward and gives the critic a lower-dimensional input better matched to the dominant dependencies. Correlated signals suffice for that, and correlation is what $\Psi$ measures. We call this second route \emph{optimization conditioning}, and Section~\ref{sec:exp-perturb} gives the reason it matters here: our randomized probes do not resolve action-level causal coupling in this demand regime, so conditioning, not preserved causation, is the reading our own data support.

H1 and H2 compose to the main hypothesis, which therefore holds only as far as both links do. Both statements are directional and neither is causal. High retention is a reason to expect a partition to do well; low retention weakens that expectation without predicting failure. Nothing in either hypothesis rules out a learner whose mechanism consumes partitions differently, and Section~\ref{sec:levels} sets up exactly such a case.

\section{Partition Construction and Selection}
\label{sec:construction}

The framework above states and measures a criterion; this section instantiates it for traffic signal control. The construction is family-first rather than algorithm-first: build the OD-induced flow graph that carries coordination pressure, partition it with a flow-based method, and let the retention score rather than the identity of the generator decide what is deployed. We use directed InfoMap as that generator (Section~\ref{sec:flowretention}), since it supplies a training-free candidate whose retained mass can be checked directly.

\subsection{OD-Demand Flow Partition Construction}
\label{sec:odconstruction}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:od-demand} makes the training-free construction explicit. The key step is to convert OD demand into a directed signal-to-signal flow matrix before any learner is trained. In simulation, the route weights come from completed SUMO trips; in a field deployment, they can come from historical assignment or a calibrated route choice model. The road graph is used to project routes and enforce connected modules, but the partitioning substrate is the OD-induced signal-transition matrix $\mathbf{W}$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
    \footnotesize
    \caption{OD-induced route-flow partition construction}
    \label{alg:od-demand}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require OD demand $\{(o,d,q_{od})\}$, routes, signals $\mathcal{V}$, feasible counts $\mathcal{K}$
        \State Initialize $W_{ij}\gets0$ for all $i,j\in\mathcal{V}$ and candidate set $\mathcal{C}\gets\emptyset$
        \For{each OD pair $(o,d,q_{od})$}
        \For{each assigned route $r$ with route share $\alpha_r$}
        \State Project $r$ to signal sequence $(v_1,\ldots,v_m)$
        \For{each consecutive pair $(v_\ell,v_{\ell+1})$}
        \State $W_{v_\ell v_{\ell+1}}\gets W_{v_\ell v_{\ell+1}}+\alpha_r q_{od}$
        \EndFor
        \EndFor
        \EndFor
        \For{each $k\in\mathcal{K}$}
        \State Run directed InfoMap on $\mathbf{W}$ and collect $k$-module candidates
        \State Keep candidates satisfying size and road-connectivity limits
        \State Add the feasible candidate with largest $\Phi_W(\mathcal{P})$ to $\mathcal{C}$
        \EndFor
        \State \Return flow-based candidate partitions $\mathcal{C}$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Completed trips contribute one unit of demand each to their realized route ($\alpha_r q_{od}=1$), while an aggregate OD table splits $q_{od}$ over its assigned routes; either way the output is a directed route-flow graph whose within-module mass is scored by Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi}.

\subsection{Coordination Mechanism Levels}
\label{sec:levels}

We hold the partition fixed while varying the coordination mechanism.
This separates the partition criterion from the learner that uses it.

\begin{table}[t]
    \centering
    \caption{Coordination levels and dependency instruments.}
    \label{tab:levels}
    \footnotesize
    \begin{tabular}{llll}
        \toprule
        Level & Mechanism          & Learner & Instrument        \\
        \midrule
        L1    & Regional reward    & IPPO    & $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ \\
        L2    & Regional critic    & MAPPO   & $D^{Q}$           \\
        L3    & Regional attention & CoLight & $D^{G}$           \\
        L4    & Regional manager   & FMA2C   & manager reward    \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

L1 uses regional reward averaging; it benefits when agents in a module have
correlated outcomes. L2 uses a regional critic; it benefits when the critic keeps the dominant state dependencies while reducing input dimension. Its instrument $D^{Q}$ is read from the centralized critic itself, so $\Psi(D^{Q})$ measures the value-relevant dependency a partition removes from the critic's input, which is the quantity L2 actually forfeits. L3 uses graph attention; it is sensitive when modules become smaller than the one-hop influence range, so the $D^G$ result is expected to expose a module-size boundary instead of always favoring flow. L4 is feudal: a manager assigns goals to the workers inside its module, so the partition fixes the manager's span of control directly. No pairwise dependency matrix is defined on an FMA2C checkpoint, and the manager's own reward, which aggregates outcomes over exactly the agents the partition grouped, is the available partition-sensitive signal.

\subsection{Retention-Guided Selection Workflow}
\label{sec:workflow}

The framework's intended use builds Flow candidates with Algorithm~\ref{alg:od-demand} alongside topology and community-detection candidates, enforces module-count, size, and connectivity constraints, estimates dependency matrices on validation traces, scores every surviving candidate by $\Phi$ and $\Psi$, selects a small validation-front subset for policy training, and reports held-out retention alongside seed-explicit training results: the final choice is made by the retention score under the operational constraints the controller must satisfy.

\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Networks and partitions}
We evaluate on two SUMO networks: Cologne8, with 8 signalized intersections and a 30-dimensional total network state, and Ingolstadt21, with 21 signalized intersections and an 85-dimensional total network state. For each network we compare one representative of each partitioning family at matched module count: directed InfoMap for the Flow candidate and METIS for the Topology candidate (Section~\ref{sec:related}), together with a reproducible size-balanced Random partition: $k=3$ on Cologne8 and $k\in\{4,5\}$ on Ingolstadt21. All main retention comparisons are matched by map and module count.

\paragraph{Retention instruments}
$D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ uses three stochastic warm-up seeds per network; $D^G$ uses one seed-7 global CoLight checkpoint per network and therefore has no checkpoint uncertainty estimate; $D^Q$ uses three independently trained global MAPPO critic seeds on Cologne8 and is not estimable on Ingolstadt21, where no completed checkpoint uses a global rather than a regional critic (Section~\ref{sec:exp-dq}). The state-MI matrices are estimated from per-agent state slices of 361-step warm-up trajectories with KSG $k=3$ and lags 1, 5, 15, and 30. Because KSG on autocorrelated traffic traces is noisy, the evidence uses partition-level retained mass and seed-wise ordering rather than isolated pairwise MI magnitudes. The learned-dependency matrices use the same partition-independent rule on both networks: take a global CoLight checkpoint, differentiate valid target outputs with respect to every source agent's input features, and aggregate absolute sensitivities into a directed non-negative matrix. A separate action-level perturbation probe (Section~\ref{sec:exp-perturb}) freezes the global CoLight policy and randomizes actions at execution time to test closed-loop causal influence without retraining.

\paragraph{Downstream training}
Downstream training evidence covers multiple independently trained seeds per (network, learner, module count) pair, covering IPPO, MAPPO, and CoLight on Cologne8 at $k=3$ and on Ingolstadt21 at $k=4$. FMA2C's hyperparameters were still being searched when these runs were collected, so its two rows (Section~\ref{sec:exp-perf}) compare partitions inside one fixed, not fully tuned, configuration rather than an optimized one. Each run is scored by its best evaluation checkpoint, the minimum delay per vehicle over its logged evaluation episodes~\citep{henderson2018matters,agarwal2021rliable}. Delay per vehicle is SUMO's time loss, the travel time accrued beyond free-flow, and is the control metric throughout. The benchmark uses 10 stochastic-policy episodes at each of 5 traffic seeds per network, split chronologically into construction, validation, and held-out test traces; every reported delay is a mean $\pm$ SD across independently trained seeds, each scored at its best checkpoint over the 5 held-out traffic seeds.

\paragraph{Random baseline}
The Random baseline is a single reproducible round-robin assignment after a seeded shuffle, so it gives a fixed chance floor rather than a distribution over random partitions.
\paragraph{Evidence standards}
We report no significance tests. The evidence is raw values, the differences between them, and the \emph{ordering} they induce, replicated across comparisons that share no seed, map, or module count. Retention scores use three traffic seeds and the seed-to-seed spread is given wherever available. Every table gives raw scores alongside the gain. Retention scores are bounded fractions, so their differences are reported in percentage points rather than ratios:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{pp}}(S)
    =
    100\,[S(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{Flow}})
        -S(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{base}})] .
    \label{eq:gainpp}
\end{equation}
Delay is an unbounded lower-is-better cost, so we report it as a relative change:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{delay}}
    =
    100\,\frac{\mathrm{Delay}_{\mathrm{Flow}}
        -\mathrm{Delay}_{\mathrm{base}}}
    {\mathrm{Delay}_{\mathrm{base}}}.
    \label{eq:waitgain}
\end{equation}
Positive $\Delta_{\mathrm{pp}}$ therefore means Flow retains more mass, while negative $\Delta_{\mathrm{delay}}$ means Flow reaches a lower delay than the named baseline.

This is a plausible optimization-conditioning mechanism, not a demonstrated cause: nothing in this study isolates size imbalance from flow-based module membership, and the cell-level directions above do not fit it consistently either, since Ingolstadt21 CoLight trains better under Flow despite carrying the study's largest module ($7$ agents).

They do, in ranking.
The informative leg is Flow against Topology, a real competing construction

all three matched map and module-count blocks and, at the seed level, in all
nine (Result 2). Both scores also rank Flow above Random in every block, but

Two qualifications follow.
First, the agreement is \emph{within} blocks and not across them.
The three paths in Fig.~\ref{fig:phi-psi} are visibly offset from one another.
The Ingolstadt21 $k=4$ flow partition retains $\Phi=0.817$ and
$\Psi=0.246$; the Cologne8 $k=3$ \emph{random} partition retains barely a
sixth as much flow, $\Phi=0.136$, and yet essentially the same dependency,
$\Psi=0.244$.
The attainable level of dependency retention is set by how many agents there
are and how many modules they are cut into, so $\Phi$ is a device for ranking
candidates within a fixed deployment, not a predictor of $\Psi$ across
networks, and they do not function as absolute quality numbers.

Second, the ordering transfers but the magnitude does not.
On Cologne8 $k=3$ the pass-through is close to one-to-one: a $32.5$ point
flow advantage buys $34.4$ points of dependency.
On Ingolstadt21 the same instrument gives $17.4 \to 2.7$ and $15.7 \to 5.3$,
a pass-through of roughly $0.16$ and $0.34$.
On the larger network most of the flow a partition additionally retains does
not appear as additionally retained state dependency.
The likely cause is the confound named in Section~\ref{sec:transfer}: common
demand correlates queues at intersections that exchange few vehicles, raising
$\Psi$ for every partition and compressing the differences between them.
This is consistent with the weaker flow--MI alignment on Ingolstadt21
($\rho=0.141$ against $0.514$) and with Topology and random tying at $k=4$.
Common demand cannot account for the whole effect, since a size-matched
random assignment would then retain comparably and does not in any block.
The compression is nonetheless real, and H1 holds as an ordering claim rather
than a proportional one.
This is the cross-check the evaluation framework needs before it can license
contribution 2's flow score as a stand-in for dependency: $\Phi$ and $\Psi$
are two different training-free scores of Section~\ref{sec:method}, and
Result 3 shows they agree on which partition to prefer in every deployment
tested here.

Under a dependency instrument read off a trained model, the relationship of Result 3 holds only in part.

At the matrix level, learned dependency agrees with physical flow better than the model-free instrument does: the Spearman correlation between $D^G$ and directed flow weight is $\rho=0.796$ on Cologne8 and $\rho=0.750$ on Ingolstadt21, against $0.514$ and $0.141$ for $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$.
What a trained attention model depends on is, pairwise, largely what the traffic does.
Yet Table~\ref{tab:psi-dg} shows the flow partition winning only two of the three blocks.

The reversal has a mechanical reading.
The analyzed CoLight checkpoints carry nonzero Jacobian influence only at hops $0$ and $1$: the model depends on its immediate neighborhood.
A partition maximizing retained corridor flow will separate two physically adjacent intersections that exchange little traffic, which costs nothing under $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ and costs a live message path under $D^G$.
As $k$ grows and modules shrink toward the one-hop radius, that trade turns against flow.
Both structured partitions stay far above Random in every block, including the reversed one, so the effect is second-order between two sensible methods.

Each $D^G$ value comes from one seed-7 checkpoint and one evaluated observation, so unlike Table~\ref{tab:psi-mi} it carries no replication.
It is computed on a global, no-partition model: a Jacobian from a partitioned model would contain cross-module zeros imposed by the partition and recover that partition trivially.
Section~\ref{sec:levels} anticipated the consequence, that a graph-attention learner does not consume a partition the way regional reward sharing and regional critics do.
This is contribution 3's first boundary condition: retained flow predicts retained dependency only for the instruments a coordination mechanism actually consumes, which is why Section~\ref{sec:exp-perf} finds CoLight's downstream benefit tracking $\Psi(D^G)$ rather than $\Phi$.

If retained dependency mattered because it preserved live causal coupling between agents, that coupling should be detectable by intervening on one agent and watching another.
We intervened and did not detect it.

The preregistered design freezes the global CoLight policy and replaces each agent's action with a uniformly random valid phase at probability $0.20$ per step (realized change rate $0.131$--$0.135$), across 400 mixed-arm and 100 single-agent episodes split chronologically into construction, validation, and held-out sets, with candidate pairs restricted in advance to physical-adjacency and observed-flow pairs at travel-time-derived lags. Action entropy rose from roughly $0.52$--$0.55$ to $0.79$ nats, confirming the intervention perturbed behavior, yet no held-out pair separated from its null and the hop-three-or-more negative controls behaved as expected ($0.00$--$0.05$), so the empty result is not a switched-off estimator; the observational CMI matrices agree, with spill-back itself nearly absent (three Ingolstadt21 events total, none on Cologne8).

Direct action-level causal coupling is therefore not resolvable at the effect sizes these data reach in this demand regime, which is not the same as its absence: an undersaturated network is where it should be smallest. The training differences reported next accordingly cannot be attributed to preserved causal coupling across module boundaries. The supported reading is the optimization-conditioning route of Section~\ref{sec:transfer}: flow-coherent modules group agents whose queues and delays are correlated, which is what $\Psi$ measures and what a regional reward average or a regional critic consumes, and correlation suffices for that mechanism whether or not a causal channel survives.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Section~\ref{sec:method}'s criterion and Section~\ref{sec:construction}'s construction meet their empirical test here: four measurements were taken on the same partitions, and the useful summary is how far they agree. Flow retention and model-free dependency retention agree completely on the informative comparison, Flow against Topology: three matched blocks out of three, nine seed-level comparisons out of nine. Both scores also rank Flow above the fixed Random floor in every block, as expected by construction rather than as independent support. Critic-level dependency retention, estimable only on Cologne8 $k=3$, agrees just as cleanly, Flow ahead of Topology in all three seed-level comparisons; that agreement extends to the one downstream MAPPO cell with a critic-level retention estimate to check it against, Cologne8's $23.6\%$ delay reduction sitting on a critic that demonstrably keeps more value-relevant input under Flow.

Downstream training agrees with the retention ordering in six of the eight matched cells (Table~\ref{tab:wait-gains}): Flow leads Cologne8 IPPO, Cologne8 MAPPO, Ingolstadt21 MAPPO, Ingolstadt21 CoLight, Cologne8 FMA2C, and Ingolstadt21 FMA2C; Cologne8 CoLight favors Topology; Ingolstadt21 IPPO is a near-tie.
The three aggregated-signal cells identified in Section~\ref{sec:exp-perf}, a regional reward mean for IPPO on Cologne8 and a regional critic input for MAPPO on both networks, are all among Flow's wins, but Flow also leads three cells whose coordination mechanism does not aggregate the partition into a single signal, Ingolstadt21 CoLight and both FMA2C cells, so aggregation is not the dividing line this table appeared to show earlier. Retention measured on a trained model's Jacobian agrees least of all, matching the flow ordering in two blocks of three and, on Ingolstadt21 at $k=5$ (single seed on both sides), pointing opposite the trained regional model's own behavior.

Our own data constrain the mechanism behind that association.
Spill-back is nearly absent from these traces, the observational CMI matrices show no action-to-queue coupling separating from its null, and a preregistered randomized intervention that demonstrably perturbed behavior found none either, so we do not attribute the training differences to preserved causal coupling across module boundaries.
The reading our evidence supports is optimization conditioning: flow-coherent modules group agents whose queues and delays are correlated, which lowers the variance of an averaged regional reward and gives a regional critic a lower-dimensional, better-matched input, a mechanism $\Psi(D^Q)$ now measures directly on Cologne8.

For deployment the procedure is concrete.
Estimate route flow from representative trips, generate feasible candidate partitions, score them by $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ on validation traces, and train only the small validation front.
The Cologne8 $k=3$ result shows why the constraints are not optional: the highest-$\Phi$ partition in the study is also the one no operator should deploy, because it reaches that score by isolating two intersections in modules of their own.
Retention ranks candidates within a feasible set; it does not define the feasible set.

The limits are as follows.
The relationship between $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ transfers in ordering but not magnitude; on the larger network most of the additionally retained flow does not appear as additionally retained dependency.
The OD matrix is measured under a random warm-up policy, before a learned controller reshapes traffic, so it describes the demand the partition was designed for, not the traffic it will eventually see. $D^G$ rests on one checkpoint per network; $D^Q$ is not estimable on Ingolstadt21, where every available MAPPO checkpoint uses a regional critic, making Ingolstadt21 MAPPO the one training cell this paper cannot cross-check against critic-level retention.
The Ingolstadt21 CoLight runs also stopped at unequal, sub-plateau episode counts.
Ingolstadt21 FMA2C's Flow--Topology gap is already smaller than either arm's own training-seed SD, so that cell's ordering is not yet distinguishable from noise; the whole-run regional reward at the same module count favors Topology (Section~\ref{sec:exp-perf}), consistent with a cell neither instrument resolves.
Section~\ref{sec:exp-phi} raises a second confound this study does not separate: whether CoLight's split cell-level outcome, favoring Topology on Cologne8 and Flow on Ingolstadt21, traces to flow-based module membership or to module-size imbalance; the same question applies to why FMA2C's Ingolstadt21 margin is an order of magnitude smaller and noisier than its Cologne8 margin, even though both now favor Flow.
A size-sequence-controlled flow partition, one that maximizes $\Phi_W$ subject to its module sizes matching Topology's multiset exactly, would isolate flow-based membership from size imbalance and is the natural next step for that question.
A higher-demand arm is the decisive next experiment for causal spill-back: the regime in which spill-back should become active and the association reported here could be tested for causation.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper introduced a training-free framework for evaluating a candidate partition before any learner is trained, instantiated that framework with a flow-based method for constructing traffic-signal coordination regions, and tested empirically whether the retention the framework measures predicts downstream MARL performance.
Building coordination modules from an OD-induced route-flow graph rather than from road geometry produces partitions that retain 15.7 to 32.5 percentage points more observed route flow and 2.7 to 34.4 percentage points more state dependency than same-$k$ Topology partitions, on every tested network, module count, and traffic seed.

The framework's value is in the relationships it exposes.
Flow retention and dependency retention rank candidates identically in every matched block, so the training-free flow score is a usable proxy for the dependency a partition preserves, though the ordering transfers while the magnitude does not.
Downstream training agrees with the retention ordering in six of the eight matched cells (Table~\ref{tab:wait-gains}); Section~\ref{sec:discussion} traces the two exceptions and shows that aggregating the partition into a single signal is not what separates Flow's wins from them. The loss is informative regardless of magnitude: the partition with the highest flow retention in the study achieves it with two singleton modules, so retention must be scored inside the deployment's size and connectivity constraints, not maximized on its own.

Two claims are withheld.
Spill-back is nearly absent in these traces and a preregistered randomized intervention resolved no action-level coupling, so the training differences are reported as consistent with optimization conditioning through correlated within-module signals, not as evidence of preserved causal coupling. No significance test is reported anywhere; the evidence throughout is the repetition of an ordering across independent comparisons (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). The next step is a higher-demand regime in which spill-back becomes active, where the association reported here could be tested for causation.
