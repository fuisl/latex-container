\begin{abstract}
    Coordinating traffic-signal agents in multi-agent reinforcement learning
    (MARL) requires partitioning intersections into coordination modules
    before training, yet existing practice either inherits partitions from
    road-topology tools or learns them end-to-end for control return, without
    measuring what a partition actually preserves.
    This paper makes three contributions.
    First, it introduces a pre-training evaluation framework for regional
    MARL traffic signal control: candidate partitions are scored by how
    much vehicle flow and inter-agent dependency they retain, and by
    whether that retention remains meaningful under size- and
    connectivity-matched null models, before any controller is trained.
    Second, it instantiates this framework with a flow-based construction
    method: a signal-to-signal flow graph built from OD trip movements
    generates candidate coordination regions, so that vehicle movement,
    rather than road geometry alone, is the basis for regional MARL
    coordination.
    Third, across two SUMO networks, flow-based partitions retain more
    route flow and state dependency than topology-based METIS partitions
    at every matched module count, and the higher-retention partition
    reaches a lower best-checkpoint delay in most matched MARL training
    comparisons, most clearly where a coordination mechanism aggregates the
    partition into a single learning signal; completed three-seed
    replication of this training gain is Cologne8-only so far, with the
    matching Ingolstadt21 comparisons pending a rerun under a revised
    training configuration.
    The exception is instructive: the partition with the highest flow
    retention in the study attains it by isolating two intersections into
    modules of their own, which is operationally unusable, so retention
    must be read under deployment size and connectivity constraints rather
    than maximized on its own.
    Randomized-intervention probes do not resolve action-level causal
    coupling in the tested demand regime, so all relationships are reported
    as measured association rather than established cause.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Urban traffic signal control (TSC) is a canonical large-scale coordination
problem: a phase decision at one intersection reshapes arrival patterns and
queue spill-back at its neighbors, so signals must be controlled jointly,
yet exact joint control requires evaluating a joint action space that grows
exponentially with the number of intersections, which is intractable beyond
a handful of
agents~\citep{nair2005ndpomdp,kok2006ndpomdp,oliehoek2012ndpomdp}.
This is why regional decomposition, not monolithic joint control, is the
paradigm that makes multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) practical at
city scale~\citep{qu2020average,qu2022scalable}: practical systems partition
the network into coordination modules, whether explicit regions or implicit
neighborhoods, and coordinate \emph{within} them, by shared rewards,
centralized critics, communication, or
hierarchy~\citep{ma2020fma2c,wei2019colight,xu2021hilight,zhu2026halo}.
Every such system thus contains a \emph{coordination graph} fixed before
training: a decision about which agents share coordination machinery.
Unlike domains where physics dictates the interaction structure, in TSC this
graph is a free design variable (Section~\ref{sec:ndpomdp}), and its design
has received far less attention than the learning algorithms that run on
top of it.

When the coordination graph is designed at all, it is typically inherited
from general-purpose partitioners applied to road \emph{topology}, learned
end-to-end for control return without stating what a good partition should
retain, or borrowed from transport-planning zoning that is not connected to
what learning agents require of a
partition~\citep{laplacian2009ieee,communitydetection2021plosone,liu2023gplight,ma2022feudal,odflow2019partition,mfdpartition2020}.
Attention-based MARL~\citep{jiang2018atoc,wei2019colight} learns interaction
structure implicitly instead, but gives no account of which dependencies are
preserved or severed when attention is restricted to modules.
The gap spanning all three lines of work is measurement: \emph{partition
    choice is consequential and routinely made, but what a given partition
    keeps or discards is almost never quantified before training}, so
competing choices cannot be compared except by running a learner on each.

This paper addresses that gap with three linked moves: a criterion, a
construction, and an empirical test of the link between them.
First, we specify a training-free criterion that scores, on the same
partitions, how much observed route flow they retain ($\Phi$) and how much
inter-agent dependency they retain ($\Psi$, under three complementary
instruments), chance-adjusted against size- and connectivity-matched nulls,
so that a partition can be ranked without first paying for a training run
(Section~\ref{sec:method}).
Second, we instantiate that criterion for traffic signal control: since
coordination pressure in traffic networks is carried by vehicle flow, an
OD-induced signal-transition flow graph is the natural partitioning
substrate, and the map equation~\citep{rosvall2008maps} supplies a
flow-based construction method on it (Section~\ref{sec:construction}).
Third, we measure how the learners that consume these partitions actually
train, and report how the three families of numbers, retained flow, retained
dependency, and training outcome, move together
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).

\noindent\textbf{Main hypothesis.}
\emph{A partition that retains more of the vehicle flow carrying
    coordination pressure retains more of the inter-agent dependency a learner
    can use, and a partition that retains more of that dependency supports
    better regional MARL.}
Every measurement in this paper is organized around that claim.
Section~\ref{sec:transfer} splits it into its two links, H1 from flow
retention to dependency retention and H2 from dependency retention to
learning outcomes, and Section~\ref{sec:experiments} tests each in turn.

Our three contributions, with evidentiary status stated explicitly:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A pre-training evaluation framework for regional MARL
              partitions} (Section~\ref{sec:method}): a training-free
          criterion that scores a candidate partition by how much observed
          route flow ($\Phi$) and inter-agent state dependency ($\Psi$,
          under three complementary instruments) it retains, chance-adjusted
          against size- and connectivity-matched null models, so that
          candidate partitions can be compared before any controller is
          trained.
    \item \textbf{A flow-based method for constructing coordination regions}
          (Section~\ref{sec:construction}): coordination modules are built
          from an OD-induced signal-to-signal flow graph over trip
          movements rather than from road adjacency alone, using the map
          equation as the partitioning objective under deployment size and
          connectivity constraints, and handed to regional reward sharing,
          regional critics, regional attention, or a feudal manager. Vehicle
          movement, not road geometry, is the partitioning substrate.
    \item \textbf{Empirical evidence linking retention to MARL performance}
          (Sections~\ref{sec:exp-phi}--\ref{sec:exp-perf}): across both
          tested networks, flow-based partitions retain more route flow and
          state dependency than matched METIS partitions at every module
          count, and higher flow retention consistently corresponds to
          higher state-dependency retention.
          The higher-retention partition also reaches a lower
          best-checkpoint delay in most matched training comparisons, nine
          of twelve in a broader single-seed sweep and, on a completed
          three-seed replication restricted to Cologne8, a large gain in two
          of three cells with the third near a tie; the matching
          Ingolstadt21 replications are pending a rerun under a revised
          training configuration (Table~\ref{tab:wait-gains}), showing that
          retained flow is a useful proxy for selecting coordination
          regions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Network partitioning for traffic control.}
Partitioning is already standard machinery in large-scale TSC, but the
criterion is almost always structural or geometric: spectral partitioning of
the road graph~\citep{laplacian2009ieee}, a manually assumed region
decomposition in feudal MARL~\citep{ma2020fma2c}, adjacency-constrained
star-topology regions~\citep{regionlight2024}, or congestion-feature and
learned-similarity
clustering~\citep{communitydetection2021plosone,liu2023gplight}.
The closest departure, \citet{ma2022feudal}, learns a flow-based
decomposition end-to-end for a feudal learner, but optimizes directly for
control return, yielding no criterion that can be stated or audited
independently of the learner.
Flow-aware partitioning exists in the transport
literature~\citep{odflow2019partition,mfdpartition2020}, but serves zoning
and macroscopic modeling, not learning agents.
Our gap claim is narrow: no prior work provides an \emph{explicit
    partition-quality criterion}, statable before training and independent of
any learner, for what a coordination-graph designer should optimize.

\paragraph{Coordination topology in MARL.}
A complementary literature learns or fixes interaction structure inside the
learner instead of the coordination graph outside it: attentional and
inferred communication~\citep{jiang2018atoc,i2c2021,peng2020structured,aamas2021correlated},
graph attention over road topology~\citep{wei2019colight}, and
information-bottleneck or scalability-oriented
topologies~\citep{hetib2025,sun2026ibvq,wang2020imac,expocomm2025,li2025topology,zhu2026halo},
which discover useful structure implicitly but give no external account of
what is preserved or severed, since it lives inside the model and cannot be
audited before training.
Our approach fixes the partition from a training-free criterion, then uses
the trained models' own dependency structure (Section~\ref{sec:psi}) to
evaluate that partition after the fact.

\paragraph{Measuring inter-agent dependency.}
We adapt three existing measurement families to a new question: model-free
mutual information between agent states, whose mechanistic link to
spill-back in TSC has been made explicit~\citep{dependencydyn2025};
Jacobian-based influence profiles from GNN and attention-attribution
diagnostics~\citep{lrd2025,abnar2020rollout,simonyan2013saliency,sundararajan2017axiomatic};
and a retention ratio that aggregates any such matrix into a partition-level
score against size-matched, connectivity-preserving nulls, which we did not
find elsewhere in the MARL literature.

\paragraph{Where the expectation comes from.}
That retained flow and retained dependency should move together is not a new
theorem here; it is assembled from cell-transmission and link-queue models
of inter-signal coupling, data-processing/information-contraction arguments,
and value-locality results in networked
MARL~\citep{daganzo1994ctm,jin2012linkqueue,polyanskiy2017sdpi,evans1999percolation,qu2020average,qu2022scalable,congeduti2021iba};
Section~\ref{sec:transfer} states the chain as H1 and H2 and names the
confound that would break each.
The map equation and Markov stability supply the flow-based partitioning
objective
itself~\citep{rosvall2008maps,rosvall2009mapequation,delvenne2010stability,lambiotte2014multiscale}.

\paragraph{Partitioning families and baselines.}
Partitioners for this task divide into two families by what they optimize.
\emph{Structure-based} methods score a cut or a density contrast on the graph
itself: normalized cut~\citep{shimalik2000},
Leiden~\citep{traag2019leiden,traag2011cpm}, and
METIS~\citep{karypis1998metis}.
\emph{Flow-based} methods score a dynamical process running on the weighted
graph, so edge weights enter as movement rather than affinity: the map
equation~\citep{rosvall2008maps} and Markov
stability~\citep{delvenne2010stability,lambiotte2014multiscale}.
Because our question is which \emph{criterion} to optimize rather than which
implementation wins, the main comparisons use one representative per family,
METIS and InfoMap.

\section{Retention Framework}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

The methodology answers two questions and is split accordingly: this section
asks what a coordination-graph partition should preserve and develops a
training-free criterion and analysis model for it, while
Section~\ref{sec:construction} asks how to build such a partition for traffic
signal control, constructing candidates from OD demand and selecting among
them under deployment constraints.
The coordination graph is a design variable rather than a given
(Section~\ref{sec:ndpomdp}), so a partition can be judged by what it preserves.
Because coordination pressure in traffic is carried by vehicle flow, we score
a partition by the OD flow it keeps inside modules, $\Phi_W$
(Section~\ref{sec:flowretention}), and by the dependency it keeps, $\Psi$,
measured with three complementary instruments and chance-adjusted against
size- and connectivity-matched nulls (Section~\ref{sec:psi}).
Section~\ref{sec:transfer} states why these two scores are expected to move
together and to track downstream learning, and names the residual effects
that would break the expectation.
Both scores are training-free: they are computed before any learner runs, so
partition quality can be ranked without first paying for a training run.

\subsection{Coordination Graph as a Design Variable}
\label{sec:ndpomdp}

Each signalized intersection $i \in \mathcal{V}$ observes local queues,
phase, and elapsed phase time $o_i$, and selects a phase action
$a_i \in \mathcal{A}_i$.
We model TSC as a network-distributed POMDP whose coordination graph
$\mathcal{G}_C=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}_C)$ determines which agents share
explicit coordination machinery.
The reward decomposes into local and interaction terms,
\begin{equation}
    R(\mathbf{a})
    =
    \sum_i r_i(o_i,a_i)
    +
    \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_C}
    r_{ij}(o_i,o_j,a_i,a_j).
    \label{eq:ndpomdp}
\end{equation}
Exact coordination over $\omega$ agents scales as $|\mathcal{A}|^\omega$,
so practical MARL systems bound module size and decide which edges to keep
before training.
In traffic, the cost of cutting an edge is governed by vehicle movement:
high-volume corridors create arrival coupling and, near saturation,
spill-back coupling.
The partitioning problem is therefore to keep the coordination graph small
while retaining the vehicle flow that carries those dependencies.

Fig.~\ref{fig:threegraphs} separates three objects that are often blurred:
the \emph{road graph} of physical adjacency, the \emph{OD-induced
    signal-transition flow graph} of vehicle exchange under a demand regime, and
the \emph{coordination graph} of agents that share rewards, critic inputs, or
messages.
Topology is not coordination pressure: two adjacent intersections with low
traffic exchange may need little explicit coordination, while a high-volume
corridor can carry dependency that an unweighted adjacency cut misses.
The partition's role is to turn the route-flow graph into a coordination
graph whose modules are small enough for MARL and coherent enough to keep
important dependencies inside modules.

The framework below therefore keeps two terms separate: the coupling term
that the retention theory bounds, and the optimization-conditioning term
that Section~\ref{sec:transfer} introduces and the experiments treat as a
leading working interpretation.

\subsection{Flow Retention and the Map Equation}
\label{sec:flowretention}
\label{sec:infomap}
\label{sec:theory}

Let $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{flow}}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E},\mathbf{W})$ be
the OD-induced signal-transition flow graph, where $W_{ij}$ is the number of
vehicles traveling from signal $i$ to the next signal $j$ along realized
routes during a representative warm-up window.
The warm-up policy is uniformly random, so the graph reflects demand and
network geometry before a learned controller reshapes the flow;
Section~\ref{sec:odconstruction} gives the construction.
For a partition $\mathcal{P}=\{P_1,\ldots,P_K\}$, define empirical OD-flow
retention as
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_W(\mathcal{P})
    =
    \frac{\sum_k\sum_{i,j\in P_k} W_{ij}}
    {\sum_{i,j} W_{ij}} .
    \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
We write $\Phi$ for $\Phi_W$ in the empirical tables.
For another non-negative edge-weight matrix $\mathbf{X}$,
$\Phi_W(\mathcal{P};\mathbf{X})$ denotes Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi} with
$\mathbf{W}$ replaced by $\mathbf{X}$.

Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi} is a scoring rule, not an algorithm: any partitioner whose
objective is defined on movement over $\mathbf{W}$ rather than on the geometry
of the cut is a candidate for scoring well under it, which is the flow-based
family of Section~\ref{sec:related}.
We work with the map equation~\citep{rosvall2008maps,rosvall2009mapequation}
because its escape term is exactly the complement of retained \emph{stationary}
flow (Eq.~\eqref{eq:qphi}), making the first link an identity rather than an
analogy, with the gap to observed OD flow carried explicitly by
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{walk}}$ below; its coding objective still only
favors, without uniquely optimizing, the flow-retentive structure this
hypothesis wants to preserve.
For the random walk used by the map equation,
\begin{align}
    L(\mathcal{P})
     & =
    q_{\circlearrowleft}H(\mathcal{Q})
    +\sum_k p_k^{\circlearrowleft}H(\mathcal{P}_k),
    \label{eq:mapeq} \\
    q_{\circlearrowleft}
     & =
    1-\Phi_\pi(\mathcal{P}),
    \label{eq:qphi}
\end{align}
where
$\Phi_\pi(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{c(i)=c(j)}\pi_iP_{ij}$ is within-module
stationary-walk mass, and $q_{\circlearrowleft}=1-\Phi_\pi(\mathcal{P})$ is
exact for this stationary edge measure
(Proposition~\ref{prop:phi}, Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).
The empirical $\Phi_W$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi} equals $\Phi_\pi$ when the OD
edge measure matches the stationary edge measure, and otherwise differs by the
measurable walk-mismatch term
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon_{\mathrm{walk}}(\mathcal{P})
    =
    |\Phi_\pi(\mathcal{P})-\Phi_W(\mathcal{P})|.
    \label{eq:walkmismatch}
\end{equation}
The entropy terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mapeq} prevent the trivial solution of one
giant module, while the measured $\Phi_W$ reports how much observed traffic
the final modules actually retain.

\subsection{Dependency Retention}
\label{sec:psi}

A partition that keeps flow should also keep the dependencies a learner can
use.
For any non-negative dependency matrix $\mathbf{D}$, define
\begin{equation}
    \Psi(\mathcal{P},\mathbf{D})
    =
    \frac{\sum_k\sum_{i,j\in P_k} D_{ij}}
    {\sum_{i,j}D_{ij}} ,
    \label{eq:psi}
\end{equation}
the fraction of measured dependency retained within modules.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:psi} is deliberately agnostic about what $\mathbf{D}$ contains:
any non-negative pairwise dependency matrix can be scored, which is what lets
the framework compare instruments that answer different questions on the same
partition.
We use three.
$D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ is lagged KSG mutual information between per-agent
traffic-state slices, not the full network state vector, over
$\tau\in\{1,5,15,30\}$ and is the model-free instrument for regional
reward sharing.
It is the right instrument for the regional-reward mechanism (level L1,
Section~\ref{sec:levels}) because independent actors have no cross-agent
input path: a Jacobian across agents would be structurally zero even if their
traffic states are highly related.
$D^{G}$ is the absolute input-output Jacobian of a global CoLight model and
audits what a trained attention model depends on.
It must be computed on a global, no-partition checkpoint; computing it on a
partitioned model would bake in cross-module zeros and then rediscover the
partition by construction.
$D^{Q}$ applies the same Jacobian rule to a centralized MAPPO critic,
measuring how strongly each agent's value estimate responds to every other
agent's state features, and is the instrument aligned with regional critics
(level L2, Section~\ref{sec:levels}).
$D^{\mathrm{CMI}}$ is conditional mutual information from one agent's action
to another's future queue, conditioning on the target's own queue and action;
it targets causal control coupling and is treated as a diagnostic, because
the effects it looks for turn out to be below what our data resolve
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-perturb}).

Because coarse or uneven partitions retain more mass mechanically, we also
use a chance-adjusted score
\begin{equation}
    \Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}
    =
    \frac{\Psi-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{R}}[\Psi]}
    {1-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{R}}[\Psi]},
    \label{eq:psiadj}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{R}$ is a null family with the same module-size sequence, and
in the benchmark also the same physical-connectivity constraint.
The former controls for size, which matters most on small networks where one
large module plus singletons scores well simply by containing many pairs; the
latter controls for the fact that any connected traffic region already
retains more dependency than an arbitrary assignment.
Raw $\Psi$ answers whether a particular deployed partition keeps dependency;
$\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}$ asks whether it keeps more than partitions under the
same size and operability constraints.
$\Phi$, $\Psi$, and $\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}$ carry the partition-level
comparison; the spill-back and action-perturbation probes enter only as
diagnostics that constrain which mechanism can be invoked to explain it.

\subsection{What We Expect Retention to Track}
\label{sec:transfer}
\label{sec:valueloss}

The main hypothesis of Section~\ref{sec:intro} is a chain: retained flow to
retained dependency to regional MARL performance.
This subsection separates it into the two links H1 and H2, states the
grounds for each, and names the confound that would produce the same reading
without the mechanism; Section~\ref{sec:experiments} tests them.
They are hypotheses rather than theorems because a formal chain from flow
retention to regional value loss needs linear flow-local dynamics, stationary
demand, and an undersaturated regime, none of which is verifiable on these
networks.

\end{figure}

\noindent\textbf{H1: flow retention tracks dependency retention.}
Across candidate partitions of the same network at the same module count, a
partition with higher $\Phi_W$ should also have higher $\Psi$, so that the
dependency it discards falls as the flow it keeps rises.
The reason is that inter-signal state coupling in traffic is carried by
vehicles: in cell-transmission and link-queue
models~\citep{daganzo1994ctm,jin2012linkqueue}, one intersection's phase
decision reaches another only by moving traffic along the corridors between
them, and information transmitted along a path attenuates with the capacity
of the links it
crosses~\citep{polyanskiy2017sdpi,evans1999percolation}.
A cut placed on low-flow pairs should therefore sever less dependency than a
cut of the same size placed without regard to flow.
The obvious confound is common demand: a shared rush-hour profile correlates
queues at intersections that exchange no vehicles, inflating $\Psi$ in a way
that has nothing to do with what the partition retains.
Section~\ref{sec:exp-relation} reports the relationship, and the
random-assignment control bounds how much of it common demand can explain.

\noindent\textbf{H2: dependency retention tracks learning outcomes.}
Among partitions of the same network at the same module count, the one that
retains more dependency should yield better regional MARL training.
Value-locality and influence-based-abstraction results for networked
MARL~\citep{qu2020average,qu2022scalable,congeduti2021iba} give the
structural version of this: a regional learner forfeits value roughly in
proportion to the interaction strength it discards.
There is also a second, non-exclusive route that does not require any
surviving causal coupling at all.
Regional reward sharing and regional critics consume the signals inside a
module as a statistical object: grouping agents whose queues and delays move
together reduces the variance of an averaged regional reward and gives the
critic a lower-dimensional input better matched to the dominant dependencies.
Correlated signals suffice for that, and correlation is what $\Psi$ measures.
We call this second route \emph{optimization conditioning}, and
Section~\ref{sec:exp-perturb} gives the reason it matters here: our
randomized probes do not resolve action-level causal coupling in this demand
regime, so conditioning, not preserved causation, is the reading our own data
support.

H1 and H2 compose to the main hypothesis, which therefore holds only as far
as both links do.
Both statements are directional and neither is causal.
High retention is a reason to expect a partition to do well; low retention
weakens that expectation without predicting failure.
Nothing in either hypothesis rules out a learner whose mechanism consumes
partitions differently, and Section~\ref{sec:levels} sets up exactly such a
case.

\section{Partition Construction and Selection}
\label{sec:construction}

The framework above states and measures a criterion; this section instantiates
it for traffic signal control.
The construction is family-first rather than algorithm-first: build the
OD-induced flow graph that carries coordination pressure, partition it with a
flow-based method, and let the retention score rather than the identity of the
generator decide what is deployed.
We use directed InfoMap as that generator (Section~\ref{sec:flowretention}),
since it supplies a training-free candidate whose retained mass can be checked
directly.

\subsection{OD-Demand Flow Partition Construction}
\label{sec:odconstruction}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:od-demand} makes the training-free construction explicit.
The key step is to convert OD demand into a directed signal-to-signal flow
matrix before any learner is trained.
In simulation, the route weights come from completed SUMO trips; in a field
deployment, they can come from historical assignment or a calibrated route
choice model.
The road graph is used to project routes and enforce connected modules, but
the partitioning substrate is the OD-induced signal-transition matrix
$\mathbf{W}$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
    \caption{OD-induced route-flow partition construction}
    \label{alg:od-demand}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require OD demand $\{(o,d,q_{od})\}$, routes, signals $\mathcal{V}$, feasible counts $\mathcal{K}$
        \State Initialize $W_{ij}\gets0$ for all $i,j\in\mathcal{V}$ and candidate set $\mathcal{C}\gets\emptyset$
        \For{each OD pair $(o,d,q_{od})$}
        \For{each assigned route $r$ with route share $\alpha_r$}
        \State Project $r$ to signal sequence $(v_1,\ldots,v_m)$
        \For{each consecutive pair $(v_\ell,v_{\ell+1})$}
        \State $W_{v_\ell v_{\ell+1}}\gets W_{v_\ell v_{\ell+1}}+\alpha_r q_{od}$
        \EndFor
        \EndFor
        \EndFor
        \For{each $k\in\mathcal{K}$}
        \State Run directed InfoMap on $\mathbf{W}$ and collect $k$-module candidates
        \State Keep candidates satisfying size and road-connectivity limits
        \State Add the feasible candidate with largest $\Phi_W(\mathcal{P})$ to $\mathcal{C}$
        \EndFor
        \State \Return flow-based candidate partitions $\mathcal{C}$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Completed trips contribute one unit of demand each to their realized route
($\alpha_r q_{od}=1$), while an aggregate OD table splits $q_{od}$ over its
assigned routes; either way the output is a directed route-flow graph whose
within-module mass is scored by Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi}.

\subsection{Coordination Coupling Spectrum}
\label{sec:levels}

We hold the partition fixed while varying the coordination mechanism.
This separates the partition criterion from the learner that uses it.

\begin{table}[t]
    \centering
    \caption{Coordination levels and dependency instruments.}
    \label{tab:levels}
    \footnotesize
    \begin{tabular}{llll}
        \toprule
        Level & Mechanism          & Learner & Instrument        \\
        \midrule
        L1    & Regional reward    & IPPO    & $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ \\
        L2    & Regional critic    & MAPPO   & $D^{Q}$           \\
        L3    & Regional attention & CoLight & $D^{G}$           \\
        L4    & Regional manager   & FMA2C   & manager reward    \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

L1 uses regional reward averaging; it benefits when agents in a module have
correlated outcomes.
L2 uses a regional critic; it benefits when the critic keeps the dominant
state dependencies while reducing input dimension.
Its instrument $D^{Q}$ is read from the centralized critic itself, so
$\Psi(D^{Q})$ measures the value-relevant dependency a partition removes from
the critic's input, which is the quantity L2 actually forfeits.
L3 uses graph attention; it is sensitive when modules become smaller than the
one-hop influence range, so the $D^G$ result is expected to expose a
module-size boundary instead of always favoring flow.
L4 is feudal: a manager assigns goals to the workers inside its module, so
the partition fixes the manager's span of control directly.
No pairwise dependency matrix is defined on an FMA2C checkpoint, and the
manager's own reward, which aggregates outcomes over exactly the agents the
partition grouped, is the available partition-sensitive signal.

\subsection{Retention-Guided Selection Workflow}
\label{sec:workflow}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
    \caption{Retention-guided coordination-graph construction}
    \label{alg:workflow}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require Trip logs, road graph, feasible module counts
        \State Build Flow candidates using Algorithm~\ref{alg:od-demand}
        \State Generate additional topology and community-detection candidates
        \State Enforce module-count, size, and connectivity constraints
        \State Estimate dependency matrices on validation traces
        \State Score candidates by $\Phi$, $\Psi$, and $\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}$
        \State Select a small validation-front subset for policy training
        \State Report held-out retention and seed-explicit training results
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:workflow} is the intended use of the framework: flow-based
and structure-based candidates are generated together, and the final choice is
made by the retention score under the operational constraints the controller
must satisfy.

\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate on two SUMO networks: Cologne8, with 8 signalized intersections
and a 30-dimensional total network state, and Ingolstadt21, with 21
signalized intersections and an 85-dimensional total network state.
For each network we compare one representative of each partitioning family at
matched module count, InfoMap flow partitions against METIS structure-based
partitions, together with a reproducible size-balanced random partition:
$k\in\{3,5\}$ on Cologne8 and $k\in\{4,5\}$ on Ingolstadt21.
$D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ uses three stochastic warm-up seeds per network; $D^G$
uses one seed-7 global CoLight checkpoint per network and therefore has no
checkpoint uncertainty estimate; $D^Q$ uses three independently trained
global MAPPO critic seeds on Cologne8 and is not estimable on Ingolstadt21,
where no completed checkpoint uses a global rather than a regional critic
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-dq}).
Downstream training evidence spans two tiers.
The primary tier (``R2'') is a three-training-seed replication covering
IPPO, MAPPO, and CoLight on Cologne8 at $k=3$ and IPPO and MAPPO on
Ingolstadt21 at $k=4$.
The second tier is a single-training-seed sweep at a 1500-episode budget,
spanning both networks, both tested module counts, and all four learners
including FMA2C.
FMA2C's hyperparameters were still being searched when these runs were
collected, so its evidence is organized as a per-configuration case study
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-perf}) rather than a seed-replicated estimate:
comparisons hold one hyperparameter generation fixed so that only the
partition differs, with a second generation on Cologne8 used as a direction
check.
Each run is scored by its best evaluation checkpoint, the minimum delay per
vehicle over its logged evaluation
episodes~\citep{henderson2018matters,agarwal2021rliable}.
Delay per vehicle is SUMO's time loss, the travel time accrued beyond
free-flow, and is the control metric throughout.
The benchmark uses 10 stochastic-policy episodes at each of 5 traffic seeds
per network, split chronologically into construction, validation, and
held-out test traces.
The state-MI matrices are estimated from per-agent state slices of 361-step
warm-up trajectories with KSG $k=3$ and lags 1, 5, 15, and 30.
Because KSG on autocorrelated traffic traces is noisy, the evidence uses
partition-level retained mass and seed-wise ordering rather than isolated
pairwise MI magnitudes.
The learned-dependency matrices use the same partition-independent rule on
both networks: take a global CoLight checkpoint, differentiate valid target
outputs with respect to every source agent's input features, and aggregate
absolute sensitivities into a directed non-negative matrix.
A separate action-level perturbation probe (Section~\ref{sec:exp-perturb})
freezes the global CoLight policy and randomizes actions at execution time
to test closed-loop causal influence without retraining.
All main retention comparisons are matched by map and module count.
The random baseline is a single reproducible round-robin assignment after a
seeded shuffle, so it gives a fixed chance floor rather than a distribution
over random partitions.
\paragraph{Evidence standards}
We report no significance tests.
The evidence is raw values, the differences between them, and the
\emph{ordering} they induce, replicated across comparisons that share no
seed, map, or module count.
Retention scores use three traffic seeds and the seed-to-seed spread is given
wherever available.
Every table gives raw scores alongside the gain.
Retention scores are bounded fractions, so their differences are reported in
percentage points rather than ratios:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{pp}}(S)
    =
    100\,[S(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{Flow}})
        -S(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{base}})] .
    \label{eq:gainpp}
\end{equation}
Delay is an unbounded lower-is-better cost, so we report it as a relative
change:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{delay}}
    =
    100\,\frac{\mathrm{Delay}_{\mathrm{Flow}}
        -\mathrm{Delay}_{\mathrm{base}}}
    {\mathrm{Delay}_{\mathrm{base}}}.
    \label{eq:waitgain}
\end{equation}
Positive $\Delta_{\mathrm{pp}}$ therefore means Flow retains more mass, while
negative $\Delta_{\mathrm{delay}}$ means Flow reaches a lower delay than the
named baseline.

The four paths in Fig.~\ref{fig:phi-psi} are visibly offset from one another.
The Ingolstadt21 $k=4$ flow partition retains $\Phi=0.817$ and

sixth as much flow, $\Phi=0.136$, and yet essentially the same dependency,
$\Psi=0.244$.

are and how many modules they are cut into, so $\Phi$ is a device for ranking
candidates within a fixed deployment, not a predictor of $\Psi$ across
networks, and they do not function as absolute quality numbers.

Second, the ordering transfers but the magnitude does not.
On Cologne8 the pass-through is close to one-to-one: a $32.5$ point flow
advantage at $k=3$ buys $34.4$ points of dependency, and $15.4$ buys $15.4$
at $k=5$.
On Ingolstadt21 the same instrument gives $17.4 \to 2.7$ and $15.7 \to 5.3$,
a pass-through of roughly $0.16$ and $0.34$.
On the larger network most of the flow a partition additionally retains does
not appear as additionally retained state dependency.
The likely cause is the confound named in Section~\ref{sec:transfer}: common
demand correlates queues at intersections that exchange few vehicles, raising
$\Psi$ for every partition and compressing the differences between them.
This is consistent with the weaker flow--MI alignment on Ingolstadt21
($\rho=0.141$ against $0.514$) and with METIS and random tying at $k=4$.
Common demand cannot account for the whole effect, since a size-matched
random assignment would then retain comparably and does not in any block.
The compression is nonetheless real, and H1 holds as an ordering claim rather
than a proportional one.
This is the cross-check the evaluation framework needs before it can license
contribution 2's flow score as a stand-in for dependency: $\Phi$ and $\Psi$
are two different training-free scores of Section~\ref{sec:method}, and
Result 3 shows they agree on which partition to prefer in every deployment
tested here.

under $D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ and costs a live message path under $D^G$.
As $k$ grows and modules shrink toward the one-hop radius, that trade turns
against flow.
Both structured partitions stay far above random in every block, including
the reversed ones, so the effect is second-order between two sensible
methods.

Each $D^G$ value comes from one seed-7 checkpoint and one evaluated
observation, so unlike Table~\ref{tab:psi-mi} it carries no replication.
It is computed on a global, no-partition model: a Jacobian from a partitioned
model would contain cross-module zeros imposed by the partition and recover
that partition trivially.
Section~\ref{sec:levels} anticipated the consequence, that a graph-attention
learner does not consume a partition the way regional reward sharing and
regional critics do.
This is contribution 3's first boundary condition: retained flow predicts
retained dependency only for the instruments a coordination mechanism
actually consumes, which is why Section~\ref{sec:exp-perf} finds CoLight's
downstream benefit tracking $\Psi(D^G)$ rather than $\Phi$.

$D^{\mathrm{MI}}$ and $D^{G}$ bracket the question this instrument settles.
The model-free instrument matches the flow ordering in four blocks of four;
the attention-policy Jacobian matches it in two.
$D^{Q}$ sits between them: model-derived like $D^{G}$, but read from the
centralized critic, the mechanism that consumes the partition at L2, so
$\Psi(D^{Q})$ measures the value-relevant dependency partitioning removes
from the critic's own input.

$D^Q_{t,s}$ is the absolute gradient mass of target $t$'s centralized critic
value with respect to source $s$'s observation features, averaged over 100
queue-load-stratified held-out observations drawn from 10 stochastic-policy
trace episodes per critic at trace seed 7001, with diagonal (self) mass
excluded.
The matrix is estimated from a global, no-partition critic before any
candidate partition is applied, so a partition cannot circularly earn credit
for dependency its own architecture forces to zero.

critic-level counterpart to check it against regardless; on that network
the optimization-conditioning reading of Section~\ref{sec:exp-perturb}
carries the cell alone.
$D^Q$ is contribution 3's most direct link between retention and
performance: the same mechanism the critic uses to train is the mechanism
$\Psi(D^Q)$ measures.

If retained dependency mattered because it preserved live causal coupling
between agents, that coupling should be detectable by intervening on one
agent and watching another.
We intervened and did not detect it.

The preregistered design freezes the global CoLight policy and replaces each
agent's action with a uniformly random valid phase at probability $0.20$ per
step (realized change rate $0.131$--$0.135$), across 400 mixed-arm and 100
single-agent episodes split chronologically into construction, validation,
and held-out sets, with candidate pairs restricted in advance to
physical-adjacency and observed-flow pairs at travel-time-derived lags.
Action entropy rose from roughly $0.52$--$0.55$ to $0.79$ nats, confirming
the intervention perturbed behavior, yet no held-out pair separated from its
null and the hop-three-or-more negative controls behaved as expected
($0.00$--$0.05$), so the empty result is not a switched-off estimator; the
observational CMI matrices agree, with spill-back itself nearly absent
(three Ingolstadt21 events total, none on Cologne8).

Direct action-level causal coupling is therefore not resolvable at the effect
sizes these data reach in this demand regime, which is not the same as its
absence: an undersaturated network is where it should be smallest.
The training differences reported next accordingly cannot be attributed to
preserved causal coupling across module boundaries.
The supported reading is the optimization-conditioning route of
Section~\ref{sec:transfer}: flow-coherent modules group agents whose queues
and delays are correlated, which is what $\Psi$ measures and what a regional
reward average or a regional critic consumes, and correlation suffices for
that mechanism whether or not a causal channel survives.

\paragraph{IPPO.}

critic that trains this policy demonstrably retains more value-relevant

matched model-derived mechanism.

single-seed sweep read well ahead of the current replication ($-48.9\%$ at

holds independent of training noise (a single-seed run read this cell as an

compare partitions inside a differently tuned learner only.
Held this way, one configuration at a time, FMA2C is directionally
consistent with the other three learners' aggregated-signal cells.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Section~\ref{sec:method}'s criterion and Section~\ref{sec:construction}'s
construction meet their empirical test here: four measurements were taken on
the same partitions, and the useful summary is how far they agree.
Flow retention and model-free dependency retention agree completely: four
matched blocks out of four, twelve seed-level comparisons out of twelve.
Critic-level dependency retention, estimable only on Cologne8, agrees just
as cleanly, Flow ahead of METIS at both module counts and in all six
seed-level comparisons; at $k=3$ that agreement extends to the one
downstream cell where a regional critic actually trains, MAPPO's R2 delay
reduction of $23.7\%$ sitting on a critic that demonstrably keeps more
value-relevant input under Flow.
Downstream training agrees least cleanly, and only three of its five matched
R2 cells are reported here.
The replicated R2 evidence shows dependency retention predicting the
training outcome in two of the three completed Cologne8 comparisons, both
mechanisms that reduce the partition to a single aggregated signal, a
regional reward mean for IPPO and a regional critic input for MAPPO; the
third, CoLight, is neutral.
The two Ingolstadt21 R2 cells, IPPO and MAPPO at $k=4$, are withheld pending
a rerun under a revised training configuration (Table~\ref{tab:wait-gains})
and are excluded from this count.
The broader single-seed sweep agrees in nine of its twelve comparisons,
including strong single-seed margins at both pending Ingolstadt21 cells that
the completed replication has not yet confirmed either way.
Retention measured on a trained model's Jacobian agrees least of all,
matching the flow ordering in two blocks of four and, on Ingolstadt21 at
$k=5$ (single seed on both sides), pointing opposite the trained regional
model's own behavior.
The pattern is the finding: what a partition retains of raw traffic and raw
state dependency predicts a regional learner's training most reliably where
that learner consumes the partition through an aggregated signal, and what
an unpartitioned model's gradients use predicts it least.

Our own data constrain the mechanism behind that association.
Spill-back is nearly absent from these traces, the observational CMI
matrices show no action-to-queue coupling separating from its null, and a
preregistered randomized intervention that demonstrably perturbed behavior
found none either, so we do not attribute the training differences to
preserved causal coupling across module boundaries.
The reading our evidence supports is optimization conditioning:
flow-coherent modules group agents whose queues and delays are correlated,
which lowers the variance of an averaged regional reward and gives a
regional critic a lower-dimensional, better-matched input, a mechanism
$\Psi(D^Q)$ now measures directly on Cologne8.

For deployment the procedure is concrete.
Estimate route flow from representative trips, generate feasible candidate
partitions, score them by $\Phi$, $\Psi$, and connected-null
$\Psi_{\mathrm{adj}}$ on validation traces, and train only the small
validation front.
The Cologne8 $k=3$ result shows why the constraints are not optional: the
highest-$\Phi$ partition in the study is also the one no operator should
deploy, because it reaches that score by isolating two intersections in
modules of their own.
Retention ranks candidates within a feasible set; it does not define the
feasible set.

The limits are as follows.
The relationship between $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ transfers in ordering but not
magnitude; on the larger network most of the additionally retained flow
does not appear as additionally retained dependency.
The OD matrix is measured under a random warm-up policy, before a learned
controller reshapes traffic, so it describes the demand the partition was
designed for, not the traffic it will eventually see.
$D^G$ rests on one checkpoint per network; $D^Q$ is not estimable on
Ingolstadt21, where every completed R2 MAPPO checkpoint uses a regional
critic, making Ingolstadt21 MAPPO the one training cell this paper cannot
cross-check against critic-level retention.
The Ingolstadt21 CoLight runs also stopped at unequal, sub-plateau episode
counts.
Seed replication covers five of the twelve legacy cells, including the two
largest single-seed effects (Cologne8 IPPO, MAPPO); extending it to
Ingolstadt21 CoLight, FMA2C, and $k=5$ on both networks remains outstanding,
and the two Ingolstadt21 replications already in hand (IPPO, MAPPO at
$k=4$) are scheduled for a rerun under a revised configuration rather than
treated as final (Table~\ref{tab:wait-gains}).
A higher-demand arm is the decisive next experiment: the regime in which
spill-back should become active and the association reported here could be
tested for causation.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper introduced a training-free framework for evaluating a candidate
partition before any learner is trained, instantiated that framework with a
flow-based method for constructing traffic-signal coordination regions, and
tested empirically whether the retention the framework measures predicts
downstream MARL performance.
Building coordination modules from an OD-induced route-flow graph rather than
from road geometry produces partitions that retain 15.4 to 32.5 percentage
points more observed route flow and 2.7 to 34.4 percentage points more state
dependency than same-$k$ METIS partitions, on every tested network, module
count, and traffic seed.

The framework's value is in the relationships it exposes.
Flow retention and dependency retention rank candidates identically in every
matched block, so the training-free flow score is a usable proxy for the
dependency a partition preserves, though the ordering transfers while the
magnitude does not.
On a completed three-training-seed replication, the higher-retention
partition trains to a markedly lower best-checkpoint delay wherever a
coordination mechanism reduces the partition to a single aggregated signal,
a regional reward mean or a regional critic input, on the busier network,
corroborated on Cologne8 by directly measured critic-level dependency
retention; the one remaining completed replicated comparison, Cologne8
CoLight, is neutral within seed dispersion, and the Ingolstadt21 IPPO and
MAPPO replications are withheld pending a rerun under a revised training
configuration rather than reported as a final result
(Table~\ref{tab:wait-gains}).
A broader single-seed sweep favors the higher-retention partition in nine of
twelve comparisons spanning regional reward sharing, regional critics,
regional attention, and a feudal regional manager, ties two more, and beats
the unpartitioned controller in nine of eleven cells that have one.
The single-seed loss is informative regardless of magnitude: the partition
with the highest flow retention in the study achieves it with two singleton
modules, so retention must be scored inside the deployment's size and
connectivity constraints, not maximized on its own.

Two claims are withheld.
Spill-back is nearly absent in these traces and a preregistered randomized
intervention resolved no action-level coupling, so the training differences
are reported as consistent with optimization conditioning through correlated
within-module signals, not as evidence of preserved causal coupling.
No significance test is reported anywhere; the evidence is the repetition of
an ordering across comparisons that share no seed, map, or module count.
The next steps are extending seed replication beyond its current five cells
to Ingolstadt21 CoLight, to FMA2C on either network, and to $k=5$ on both
networks; rerunning Ingolstadt21 IPPO and MAPPO under a revised training
configuration (Table~\ref{tab:wait-gains}); and a higher-demand regime in
which spill-back becomes active and the association reported here could be
tested for causation.
